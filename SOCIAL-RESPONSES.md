# Social Responses — Reply Templates

Starter language for replying to Facebook comments on shared journal pieces. Adapt to each commenter — these are templates, not scripts.

**Related:** [SOCIAL-SHARE-PLAN.md](./SOCIAL-SHARE-PLAN.md) (the share sequence), [RESEND-PLAN.md](./RESEND-PLAN.md) (email outreach drafts).

**When to use this doc:** After any public share of a journal piece, when comments start coming in. Pull up on mobile, find the right template, personalize, post.

---

## Rules for replying

- **Reply to every comment**, even with one line. People notice when you don't.
- **Use the commenter's first name.** It signals you actually read it.
- **Don't use the same reply twice in a row.** Vary the language even when the sentiment is the same.
- **Don't go heavy on a light comment.** Match the energy.
- **Don't apologize for the silence in replies.** You did it once in the post. Don't keep doing it in comments.
- **For "I've been there" replies, do not pivot to advice.** Acknowledge, thank, stop.
- **Skip emojis** unless the commenter used them first and it would feel cold to drop them.
- **Long, emotional comments from close people** → reply briefly online, then follow up off-platform (text, coffee, call). Don't process publicly what belongs in private.

## Templates by comment type

### "So glad you're back / so brave / praying for you" *(acquaintance well-wishers)*

> Thank you, [Name]. Means a lot.

> Appreciate you, [Name]. Quietly back at it.

> Thanks, [Name]. Good to hear from you.

---

### "I had no idea you went through all this" *(surprised friend)*

> Yeah — it was a hard year. I didn't say much while it was happening. Glad to be on this side of it. Thanks for reading.

> Thanks, [Name]. Most of this didn't get told in real time. I'm grateful you read it now.

---

### "Thank you for writing this — I've been there" *(fellow sufferer — most important to reply thoughtfully)*

> Thanks, [Name]. Means a lot to hear that from someone who knows. I'm grateful you read it.

> [Name] — that means more than you know. Hope you're doing okay these days.

> Thank you, [Name]. Knowing someone else recognized themselves in it makes it easier to have written.

**Do not:** pivot to advice, recovery talk, or recommendations. Acknowledge, thank, stop.

---

### People who knew you during the silence *(Patreon, ministry, farm patrons)*

> [Name] — thank you for sticking around through the quiet stretch. That mattered more than I said at the time.

> Thanks, [Name]. You were one of the people who didn't disappear on me. I haven't forgotten.

---

### Comments addressed to Heather

> She's the reason I'm still here. I'll pass it on.

> Heather is the steady one. I'll tell her you said that.

---

### Long, emotional comments from close people

> [Name] — I read this twice. Thank you for being there during the part I didn't write about. Let's get coffee soon.

> [Name] — this is too much for a comment thread. Texting you.

**Then actually follow up off-platform.** The reply is a placeholder; the relationship is the work.

---

### Light "welcome back / good to see you" surface comments

> Thanks, [Name]. Good to be posting again.

> Appreciate it, [Name].

---

### Comments from people you haven't seen in years

> [Name] — it's been a while. Thanks for reading. Hope you and yours are well.

---

### "You should write a book" / "this should be in a magazine"

> Thanks, [Name]. Slowly working on it.

> Appreciate it, [Name]. There's a longer version coming, eventually.

**Don't oversell the book or the publication plan in a comment thread.** Quiet acknowledgment is enough.

---

### Negative or dismissive comments *(rare, but possible)*

Default move: don't reply. Let it sit. The post stands on its own.

If a reply is genuinely warranted (the comment misrepresents something factual):
> Thanks for reading, [Name]. *(Then a single sentence of correction. No more.)*

Do not engage in back-and-forth. One reply, then stop.

---

## What this doc is not

- Not a substitute for honest replies. If a template doesn't fit a comment, write something specific. Templates are starting language, not finishing language.
- Not a script for DMs or email. Those are warmer and longer — see `RESEND-PLAN.md` for email drafts and personal-touch sequences.
- Not for replies on professional / Headley Web posts. Those have a different voice. This doc is for journal-piece comments only.
