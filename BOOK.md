## What Kind of Book This Is

Not a memoir with profile chapters bolted on. Not a journalism collection with a personal intro.

The closest analog: a **literary memoir in which the author's healing and the subjects' stories are the same argument**. The memoir chapters raise a question; the profile chapters answer it — not abstractly, but through specific people in the same geography, at the same moment.

The reader finishes a chapter about the hospital, or the farm sale, or the silence, and then reads about Samuel Sawyer building a hydroponic farm in downtown Anniston after his own failures — and the juxtaposition does the work the author doesn't have to say out loud.

## The Structural Logic

**Memoir chapters** track the arc of collapse and rebuild — chronologically, emotionally, honestly.

**Profile chapters** are placed *not randomly* but at the point in the memoir where the subject answers the question the memoir just raised. The logic is:

- Memoir raises: *What does it mean to lose what you built?*
- Profile answers: Here is someone who also lost what they built, in this same county, and kept going anyway.

**The profiles are not inspiration porn.** They don't exist to prove recovery is possible or to cheer the reader up after a hard chapter. They exist because the question "what kept them going?" is genuinely asked by someone who wanted to keep going and couldn't — and found his way back partly by watching other people build.

**The reader doesn't need the author to explain the connection.** The placement IS the argument.

**CHAPTER: manic-episode.md**
*(The mania that felt holy: 16-20 hour days, Heather overwhelmed while Matt felt amazing, friends naming "manic Matt" before he could, dozing at the wheel)*

**Question raised:** What does it feel like when the most alive you've ever been is actually a symptom? When what feels like calling can't be trusted?

**Profile:** **Aquality Farms (Samuel Sawyer)**

**Logic:** The same physical space. Matt and Samuel were business partners at the Anniston farmers market — the exact location where Matt was scaling everything during mania. Samuel showed up at the same market every Saturday with coolers and no booth reputation, five years of research behind a napkin, and built something that now feeds 35,000 students a week. Matt was performing holy-feeling confidence at the same market, working 16-20 hour days, passing out business cards. Samuel was quietly preparing for years before anyone knew what they were watching start. The contrast is not stated — it's placed. The question the memoir raises ("what does holy-feeling confidence look like from the outside?") is answered by Samuel's portrait of methodical, quiet preparation. Also: Samuel says explicitly that every failure in his life made him stronger. He says it like he's already tested it.

**Connective tissue seed needed:** ✗ *Not written yet.*

> *Seed direction:* A paragraph or two that moves the reader from Matt's body — dozing at the wheel on the way to Walmart — to the farmers market. The market as a specific place: the coolers, the mushrooms, the people lining up to ask questions. The camera pulls back from Matt's interiority to Samuel across the aisle, already there before Matt arrived, still there after everything came apart. Not stated. Placed.

**CHAPTER: becoming-a-web-designer.md**
*(Solitaire in the camper. Coding class. Thirty minutes in the car outside Called Coffee before going in. Cold calls. Getting hung up on. First client. "I started feeling excited to wake up in the morning.")*

**Question raised:** What does starting over look like when you've run out of identity? What does "excited to wake up" mean after everything?

**Profile:** **Jean Ellison (Mom-To-Go)**

**Logic:** Two people from JSU who both hit the floor, at different times and in different ways, and are both building now with the specific knowledge of what it feels like when the ground isn't solid. Jean's floor literally moved — "I thought I was on a boat. Literally. I couldn't walk." Matt sat in his car outside Called Coffee for thirty minutes trying to figure out how long he'd stay inside. Both are rebuilding from the specific knowledge of failure. Jean says it plainly: *"Welcome to the club."* Her ambition isn't softened — *"I want to be a millionaire, Matt. Like, I'm not even playing."* That energy — explicit, loud, earned — is what the rebuild chapter needs after it. Not inspiration. Just two people who know what the floor feels like, building anyway.

The personal connection also earns the placement: Matt has known Jean for twenty years. They were at JSU together. He told her about his year during the interview. She didn't offer a platitude. She leaned in.

**Connective tissue seed needed:** ✗ *Not written yet.*

> *Seed direction:* The transition from the first client (Giovanna) into Jean's profile needs a paragraph that earns the jump — something about what it means to call someone you've known for twenty years and ask to tell their story. **CHAPTER: faith-journey-raw-notes.md**
*(20 years of vocation. Marcus Borg's postcritical naivete. Liminal space. Deconstruction without reconstruction. "I'm just hanging out in the margins." Not resolved. Not landed.)*

**Question raised:** What do you do when the identity that held everything is gone, and you're not sure you want to rebuild it the same way?

**Profile:** **Interfaith Ministries**

**Logic:** *"I'm no longer at First UMC, but the work next door hasn't stopped."* That line is the connective tissue. Matt spent four years next door to Interfaith without fully understanding what it did. The faith-journey essay is about 20 years of vocational identity ending — the grief of a calling that no longer exists in the form it used to take. Interfaith answers it: the mission outlasts the chapter. The coalition has held for 50 years across Baptist and Catholic, Black and white, Christian and Jewish — people with real theological differences who decided that helping their neighbors mattered more. Matt lost his vocation. The work it pointed toward didn't stop. The churches kept giving, the volunteers kept showing up. That's not a resolution. It's a different frame for what remains.

The profile also contains the harder line: *"That's not a situation where spiritual counsel helps. It's a situation where money helps."* Matt built his life on being the person with spiritual counsel. Interfaith is where the people he couldn't help went. The essay and the profile together are the book's most honest reckoning with what vocation actually costs.

**Connective tissue seed needed:** ✗ *Not written yet.*

> *Seed direction:* The pivot from faith essay into Interfaith profile needs almost nothing — just the geography. Four years next door. The profile does the work.

## What the Book Is Not

- A recovery memoir with a happy ending
- An inspiration story about the South
- A business book about building a freelance agency
- A Christian memoir about faith surviving hard seasons

All of those readings are available. None of them is the book.

The book is about what it means to be the person with answers who ran out of them — and what you find when you stop performing strength long enough to watch other people build.

## Strategic Note on Timeline

*(For when this question comes up: what does "5-year arc" mean?)*

The 3–5 year arc refers to the *return on investment* from a published book — agent, publication, ministry legacy impact, speaking invitations. Not writing time. Not healing time.

**Being inside the arc is an argument for writing sooner, not later.** Material written from inside an experience has a quality that can't be reconstructed after it's resolved. The roughness of the faith-journey transcript — "I'm just hanging out in the margins," not knowing where the faith essay lands — that unresolvedness IS the material. A book written in five years from a place of full resolution will be a different book, and probably a less honest one.

**Write it for healing first. Write it for ministry second.** Let the business payoff be whatever it is.

The Southern Legends site is the book in serial form. The profiles and journal entries aren't blog content — they're chapters finding their shape. The book is already being written. This document is about what it becomes.
