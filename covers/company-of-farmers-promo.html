Company of Farmers

The people who taught me to love the land — and the farm I dreamed, built, and did not keep.
By Matt Headley · former pastor, flower farmer, Northeast Alabama

Before I had a farm, I had a drawer full of farmers. Business cards. Dog-eared books. Names in the margins of vision documents I wrote at two in the morning. Then I lost the farm.

This book is about both at once — the ones who crossed the water I was still standing in, and the man who reached for the same ground and came up short. Half portrait. Half confession.

Each chapter is a farmer. Each farmer is a mirror.

Wendell Berrythe century. Place as fidelity. You belong to a piece of ground the way you belong to a marriage.
Joel Salatinthe megaphone. The difference between conviction and performance — learned mostly by watching myself blur the line.
Justin Rhodesthe homecoming. Back to the grandfather's land, broke and sick, and filming it anyway.
Noah Sandersthe doctrine. Goodwater, Alabama. The ground is God's; farming is worship. I heard him say it out loud in 2018.
Cale & Kelly Crosbythe proof. The whole theology lived one county over, kids underfoot — close enough they drove to my morning prayer.

Read the chapters as they land.

The early chapters are already up as Southern Legends profiles. The rest come one farmer at a time. I'll email you the next one.

No pledge, no "support my writing." Just the next farmer when the next farmer is ready.
