## Summary

Matt is drafting a new journal entry for Southern Legends that addresses a foundational question: *Why does personal vulnerability and mental health processing belong on a website designed as a top-of-funnel strategy for Headley Web?*

**Key Insights:**
- The real answer is relational: Matt is wired for connection and community, not isolation. He lost his pulpit when the church and flower farm community dissolved. Southern Legends—and its mix of business profiles and personal journaling—is how he's reclaiming that community.
- The jealousy and comparison that delayed profiling for months is worth naming. It's honest and human.
- The profiles of Samuel Sawyer and Jean becoming wins (shared, commented on, celebratory) while in isolation showed him that connection was possible again.
- Public processing matters because it invites others to meet him where he is, rather than pretending to have it figured out.
- The journal entries aren't a liability to lead gen—they're proof that the person writing profiles is real, still in process, and trustworthy.

**Structure for the post (skeleton):**
1. **Opening:** "I missed people. I've been silent for six months. I lamented that silence even while I was doing it."
2. **The isolation:** Little John Road walks, farms and gardens he lost, the flower farm, church community, Called Coffee, Samuel and Justin.
3. **The weight of it:** Psych ward, weight gain, torn meniscus, hard feelings with Heather about what ended.
4. **The turn:** Profiles of Samuel and Jean as wins when there were no other wins. Comments and shares feeling like connection again.
5. **Why this matters:** Southern Legends is how he came back. The profiles were the door. The journal proves it's real.
