Indigo Ridge Farms sits on Merrill Harper Road in White Plains, in the fold of the southern Appalachian foothills just north of Anniston. Cale and Kelly Crosby raise pasture-raised chicken and pork there, grass-fed and grass-finished beef, rabbit, eggs, honey, and whatever else the season gives. They registered the farm as an LLC in the fall of 2022. They are certified grass-fed by the American Grassfed Association, they belong to Sweet Grown Alabama, they produce through the Alabama Farm Co-op, and they haul their meat to Pepper Place in Birmingham on market mornings.

That is the farm. Here is the claim underneath it.

The Crosbys say plainly that they were "put on this planet together to fulfill their God-given responsibility of being good stewards to both land and livestock." That is not marketing language, or it is not only marketing language. It is a theological statement about what land is for and to whom it belongs. Most farms that sell you grass-fed beef are selling you a health outcome or a taste. The Crosbys are selling you those too, but they lead with a doctrine. They believe the ground is a trust, that the animals are a charge, and that farming is a form of obedience. They believe the land is a covenant.

---

## The Return

Kelly did not grow up putting up hay. Before Indigo Ridge, she worked as a family nurse practitioner for CommonSpirit Health in the Mountain Region, the high country of Colorado, a long way from Calhoun County. That is the part of the story the American Grassfed certificate does not tell you.

There is a pattern here worth naming, because it is the same shape as [Justin Rhodes going back to his grandfather's land](/journal/justin-rhodes-profile) and the same argument [Wendell Berry](/journal/wendell-berry-profile) has been making for fifty years. The people who take a specific piece of ground most seriously are very often the ones who left, learned something out in the wider world, and came back on purpose. The Crosbys spent a chapter out West. They came home to the foothills and bought dirt. The returning is not incidental to the theology; it is the theology. You cannot keep covenant with a place you are only passing through.

They are doing it [Salatin-style](/journal/joel-salatin-profile), moving the animals, stacking the enterprises, letting the chickens follow the cattle, treating the pasture as the actual product and the meat as what the pasture produces. And they are doing it with their kids underfoot, which is the part no certification measures and the part that matters most. The children are not a lifestyle detail. They are the reason the word "generations" keeps showing up in how the Crosbys talk. Kelly had a baby last year. You do not raise your infant on ground you intend to strip.

---

## Why It's Different

There are working farms all over Northeast Alabama, and a lot of them are good. Some of them are in this directory. What separates Indigo Ridge is not the product line, plenty of people sell pastured chicken. It is that the Crosbys have organized a whole family's life around a proposition about creation, and they will tell it to you if you ask.

That proposition is unfashionable in two directions at once. To the industrial food system, treating soil as a living thing you are answerable to is sentimental and inefficient. To a certain kind of secular regenerative-ag crowd, naming God as the one you are answerable to is embarrassing. The Crosbys are not troubled by either. They think the health of the meat and the health of the soil and the health of the family are the same subject, and that the subject has an author. It is a coherent position. It is also, quietly, a countercultural one.
