There's a concept in urban sociology called the third place — not home, not work, the other place where a community actually happens. The coffee shop. The barbershop. The corner where people linger past their reason to be there.

Jerod Horsley knew Anniston well enough to know it was missing one.

Called Coffee opened in downtown Anniston and filled that gap with intention. The name is not accidental. Called — as in vocation, as in the thing you do because you are supposed to do it, not because it makes easy sense. Coffee shops in small Alabama cities do not automatically succeed. The ones that do succeed usually have someone behind them who understood the risk and showed up anyway.

---

The shop sits in a stretch of downtown that has been having a longer conversation about itself. Anniston is a city with the bones of something — civic architecture, proximity to the mountains, a creative class that has been slowly coalescing for a decade around places like this one. Called Coffee has become one of the places around which that coalescing happens.

River, who runs the floor, is the kind of person who remembers you. That matters in a third place. The difference between a coffee shop that feels like a community and one that feels like a transaction is almost always one person who makes people feel recognized.

---

Jerod built the space with a specific city in mind. Called Coffee is not a chain concept transplanted somewhere — it belongs to the block it's on, to the conversations it has hosted, to the people who have made it a habit.

Some businesses in small cities are waiting for the city to change before they can be what they want to be. Some decide not to wait.

Called Coffee is the second kind.
