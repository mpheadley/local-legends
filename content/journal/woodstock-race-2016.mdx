---

Woodstock Avenue at seven in the morning. The heat already coming up off the pavement. A thousand people who chose to be awake for this.

There is always a national anthem first. Somebody sings it — sometimes well, sometimes not, always with full intent. Then the blast horn, and the pack pushes forward, and another Woodstock 5K is underway.

I know this because I have run several of them. I know Ellen Cannon, who is always there helping. I know John DiMartino, who runs it every year. I know that Nancy Grace is the oldest and runs it like a legend, and that Brooke Nelson runs all of them, every single one. I know what the start line looks like when it is packed with competitive spirit and people who woke up early because they wanted to.

I know the bird. Woodstock, the little yellow one — he is on all the shirts. I have shirts from years I ran and years I only watched. You know the race by the bird.

---

This particular year, I started at the back of the line.

Not because I had to. Because I was pushing a double running stroller with both of my twins inside, and it seemed right. Sage and Soren were seven months old. The kind of small where they still fit in the stroller without argument, where they had no opinion about where they were going, where the world was just whatever was passing in front of their eyes and the motion of being carried.

Their cheeks would bounce on the hills.

I know this because I was watching them. You push a double stroller up a hill and you watch the cargo, and what I saw was Sage and Soren's cheeks, bouncing with each step, their faces alert and unconcerned, passengers in something they had not signed up for.

Some of the runners going by had opinions about this.

*You've got to be kidding me.*

Said in a tone that was not quite criticism and not quite admiration. The tone of someone watching a thing happen that they did not expect to see at a 5K in Ohatchee and are not sure whether to be amused or alarmed. I took it as a compliment. I started at the back. I ended up passing some of them on the hills.

Sub-thirty minutes. Not bad for a man with a double stroller and two passengers with bouncing cheeks.

---

Hannah and Noah ran the 1K fun run. This is the part I think about most.

I ran the race. They ran their race. We were all on Woodstock Avenue that morning — my four kids, all of us doing the thing that was sized for us. Hannah at nine, Noah at eight, doing a kilometer. Sage and Soren in the stroller, doing whatever you call it when you are seven months old and along for the ride.

The *Anniston Star* was there. They photographed the race. I know this because we were in it — me and the stroller and the twins, the picture that exists somewhere in the Star archive of a man who started at the back and pushed up the hills.

I was so proud of my kids.

---

The Woodstock 5K has been going for forty-five years. In forty-five years a lot has closed around Ohatchee and Anniston — the Army post, the mills, things you don't replace. The race kept going.

It benefits the Arc of Calhoun and Cleburne Counties — Patti Tiller's organization, the same one that showed up at Shepherd's Table. *(Look for the picture on the Interfaith profile — her and Matt and the Arc volunteers, January 2025.)* The race and the ministry and the community are connected the way things in small Alabama cities are connected: by the same people showing up in different rooms.

---

This year I am a vendor. Southern Legends. That is what changed.

I used to be a runner and a watcher. Now I have a table.

I still have the shirts. The bird is on all of them.

---

My kid. So proud. I ran with them.
