Before I had a farm, I had a drawer full of farmers.

Business cards. Dog-eared books. Names written in the margins of vision documents I wrote at two in the morning during years I now understand better than I did then. Between 2022 and 2025 I was building a flower farm with my wife on nine acres near the Chief Ladiga Trail, and I was also collecting people who had already done the thing I was reaching for. I read them the way you read someone who has crossed the water you are still standing in.

Then I lost the farm.

This book is about both of those things at once, because they were never really separate. It's a book about the men and women who taught me to love the land, and it's a book about a man who dreamed the ground, worked it, and did not keep it. Half portrait. Half confession.

Each chapter is a farmer. Each farmer is also a mirror.

- **[Wendell Berry](/journal/wendell-berry-profile)** gave me the century — place as fidelity, the idea that you belong to a piece of ground the way you belong to a marriage.
- **[Joel Salatin](/journal/joel-salatin-profile)** gave me the megaphone, and taught me the difference between conviction and performance, mostly by making me watch myself blur the line.
- **[Justin Rhodes](/journal/justin-rhodes-profile)** gave me the homecoming — going back to the grandfather's land, broke and sick, and filming it anyway.
- **[Noah Sanders](/profiles/noah-sanders)** gave me the doctrine. I drove down from Weaver to his *Redeeming the Dirt* conference — a camp in Coosa County, October 2018 — still a pastor, still years from my own dirt, and heard a man from Goodwater say out loud that the ground is God's and farming is worship.
- **[Cale and Kelly Crosby at Indigo Ridge](/journal/indigo-ridge-farms-profile)** gave me the proof — the whole theology being lived one county over, with kids underfoot, close enough that they drove to my morning prayer.

I am not writing a how-to. I am the last person who should tell you how to keep a farm; I didn't. This is a book about *why the land pulls at a certain kind of person* — and what it costs to answer the pull, or to answer it and still come up short. The credential here is the wreckage, not the podium. I think that's the honest version. I know it's the only one I'm able to write.

The early chapters are already here, as [Southern Legends profiles](/profiles). The rest are coming, one farmer at a time.

*Matt Headley is a former pastor, flower farmer, and classically trained singer from Northeast Alabama. His memoir [*Broken Ground*](/essays/forthcoming-broken-ground) is about the losing; this book is about the company he kept along the way.*
