The posts were 6x12s.

I know this because I carried them. July in Alabama, torn meniscus, forty-two years old, and I was carrying 6x12 posts across a yard in Jacksonville and driving screws into deck boards for eight hours a day. My shoulder bruised somewhere in the second week. I stopped noticing it around day ten.

I had an M.Div. I had been ordained for thirteen years. I had preached several hundred sermons.

Deck boards.

---

The math I kept doing was simple and pointless. Twenty years of accumulated expertise in pastoral care, liturgy, community formation, homiletics, staff management, nonprofit administration, and something called missional ecclesiology — weighted against what I was actually doing, which was sweating into a shirt I was going to have to wash that night because I only brought three. The math never came out. I kept doing it anyway. It's the kind of arithmetic you do when you are trying to understand how you arrived somewhere and the answer won't hold still.

I had bought a Tacoma. I knew by July that I would have to sell it back.

---

Jacksonville is not a neutral place. I went to college at Jacksonville State. I knew streets there by their sounds at night. There were people in that town who had watched me preach, watched me lead worship, watched me try to be someone useful. I drove through it every morning with lumber in the truck bed and tried not to think about that.

I mostly failed.

---

The thing about physical labor at forty-two with a bad knee is that it is honest in a way that nothing else is. Your body has no patience for abstraction. It does not care what you used to do or what you meant to do or how the office that was supposed to be yours ended up with someone else's name on it. It only wants you to pick up the board and put it where it goes.

I picked up the board. I put it where it went.

I finished in August. Drove home the way I'd been driving home all month — through Jacksonville, past the campus, past the roads I knew by name. The shoulder had stopped hurting. The meniscus was still there, doing what it did.

The deck was good. I built it well.
