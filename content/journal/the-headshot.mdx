I have a folder on my computer called `headshots-stylized`.

Inside that folder: a hedcut engraving, the kind newspapers used to use for notable contributors, black and white, cross-hatching that suggests gravitas. A Ghibli watercolor portrait — me as an animated character in a cowboy hat, looking at something off-frame. A portrait in the style of old oil paintings, the kind hung in libraries. Me in charcoal. Me in woodcut.

And a Microsoft Paint portrait of a bald man with an expression that could be a smile or could be a warning.

This is my official headshot. I am a professional.

---

The hedcut exists because I have a design brief. Not because I sit for portrait sessions — that would be normal. I have a system. I describe my face to an AI and it renders what I might look like if someone had paid attention. The Ghibli portrait goes on content that is warm, community-focused, theological. The hedcut goes on business materials and anything with the word "Blueprint" in the title. There is a document that specifies which photo to use for which type of social post. It is one section inside a larger document that is roughly four thousand words long.

I wrote it on a Tuesday because I was building in public.

---

"Building in public" means you show your work as you make it. The scaffolding visible. The process documented. I have adopted this as a personal operating principle because I believe radical transparency is how trust gets built in the age of disclosed AI, and also because I have four children and no marketing budget.

The four thousand words govern what my AI assistant is allowed to post, what I am allowed to spend, how I should be described to machines, and whether my wife needs to sign off on purchases over fifty dollars. I wrote it iteratively, the same way I used to write sermons: adding rules as situations arose, always slightly behind the need.

One section describes my humor as "dry and incidental, never announced."

The MS Paint portrait announces everything.

---

The file is named `20260425_174024_mspaint_mspaint-portrait-of-a-bald-man-with-an-e.` It ends mid-word. Something was truncated. The eyes are doing different things. The smile is technically present. The man in the portrait is bald. All of this is also true of me, so in that sense it is accurate.

I don't remember when it was made, or what sequence of decisions led to its creation and subsequent placement in `headshots-stylized` alongside the professional assets.
