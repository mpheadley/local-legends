The last person to buy flowers from the Headley Flower Farm was Tammy Jackson.

It was the summer of 2025. The farm was weeks from closing. Tammy ordered through Heather — directly, not through me. The flowers were for her first granddaughter's baptism.

She did not know the farm was closing. She bought them because her granddaughter was being baptized and she wanted flowers. That is all.

I did not know she had bought them. That October I was in a psychiatric center at UAB. I would not find out about the flowers until much later.

---

In November she texted. She had been praying for me, she said. She did not want to intrude. She said she missed seeing me, and that was not pressure to come back to church — just acknowledgment that I was cared about and had made real contributions.

She had not needed to send that text. There was no obligation. The professional relationship had ended. She sent it anyway.

When I eventually told her about the bipolar diagnosis — that I had been in a manic state when I resigned, that I was sorry for what my behavior had cost her and the staff and the congregation — she wrote back:

*My concern has always been your health and wellbeing. The job was entirely secondary.*

She offered her apology again. She had apologized once before. She offered it a second time.

*The apology is gratefully received but unneeded.*

That sentence takes a long time to earn. You have to have been through something to write it without performing it. You have to have processed the hard part and come out the other side into a place where the grace is just what's left.

---

Tammy Jackson has been the senior pastor at Anniston First United Methodist Church for years — leading one of the oldest congregations in the city through a stretch of institutional uncertainty that would have broken a less settled person. I am not the right person to write that profile. I was her associate pastor. I did not see her whole life, only the part of it that overlapped with mine.

What I saw: a woman who handled a situation she was not equipped for — because no one is equipped for what I put her through — and who, when the language finally arrived for what had happened, responded with grace that did not demand anything back.

The flowers are not a metaphor.

She bought them because her granddaughter was being baptized. The farm happened to still be open. Heather took the order and cut the flowers and they went to a baptism.

But the loop it closes is something I have been sitting with for a long time. The pastor who managed the hardest year of my time in ministry, who texted in November with genuine warmth, who received my apology without needing it and offered her own again — she is the one who bought the last flowers.

Not a metaphor. Just what happened. Just grace, doing what grace does, without announcing itself.

---

*Tammy Jackson is the senior pastor at Anniston First United Methodist Church in Anniston, Alabama.*
