Dr. Nathan Wight ran the Jacksonville Opera Theatre at Jacksonville State University through the 2000s. He directed the productions. He set the standard.

The standard was serious. He staged *Hansel and Gretel* (Humperdinck, January 2007), *The Pirates of Penzance* (Gilbert and Sullivan, May 2007), *The Mikado* (2008), *Sweeney Todd* (Sondheim, 2008) — not as student recitals but as full opera productions with costumes, sets, orchestra, and a trained cast. Students were expected to learn the language of the libretto, develop a character, and perform at a university level.

I was in those productions. He cast me as the Father in *Hansel and Gretel*. He cast me — or I auditioned into — *Pirates* and *Sweeney Todd*. Working under him meant working seriously. There was no coasting. The Stone Center stage held an audience; the productions earned it.

What he built at JSU gave a regional university a genuine operatic program — training singers who went into churches and school programs and community stages across Northeast Alabama. That kind of program is not a given at a small university in Calhoun County. He built it and maintained it at a level that mattered.
