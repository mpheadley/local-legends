Woodstock 5K ● Writing Prompts

Forty-Five Summers · SL profile · you write the openings, the ending, the “I”
Opening — you write

Put us on Woodstock Avenue before the gun

The heat already coming up off the pavement at 7 a.m. A thousand people who chose to be awake for this. One scene, no summary. Don’t tell us it’s old yet — show us the morning. If you’ve run it, start there, first person.

Middle — the real subject

What it has outlasted

45 years in a town that lost its mills and its Army post. The race kept going. Report what closed around it. The universal question underneath: what keeps a thing going when the town around it kept losing things? Asked by someone who built something and couldn’t keep it.

Reporting gaps — go find these

The people

  • Who runs the Anniston Runners Club now? Who’s set the cones the longest?
  • The oldest runner — the one who’s run 30+ of these. What do they look like crossing the line?
  • Why the Arc of Calhoun & Cleburne? Who chose that beneficiary — any runner or family with a stake?
  • First sensory hit: what does the start line look/sound/smell like at 7:25 a.m.?
Merch — the canary

Work the bird in

45th-year mark is a canary — bright, small, stubborn, a bird that sings underground. On the shirt with the race name and year. The merch is a fundraiser, licensed through the club, cut back to the Arc. One clean line somewhere, not a sales pitch.

Ending — you write

End on an image or a fact, not a lesson

The last runner across the line, volunteers already stacking cones. Or a kid in the Kidstock 1K. You lost a thing you built — sit with what it means to watch a town keep one going. Report it. Trust the reader.
