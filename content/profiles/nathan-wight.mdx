There is a kind of work that requires you to believe in something the institution has not fully committed to yet. You mount a full opera production at a public university in northeast Alabama — real staging, real costumes, a live pit orchestra, an opening night — and you have to believe that it matters before you can convince anyone else that it does.

Nathan Wight believed it.

He directed opera at Jacksonville State University, putting up productions in the Ernest Stone Performing Arts Center that were actual operas: *The Pirates of Penzance*, *Hansel and Gretel*, *The Mercato*. Not operettas. Not excerpts. Full productions.

---

## What It Means to Stage an Opera

A full opera production requires more moving pieces than a department is usually willing to coordinate. You need singers who can act and actors who can sing, which are not the same thing. You need a pit orchestra. You need costumes, sets, lighting, stage direction.

And you need singers who are prepared — prepared enough to hold a dramatic role for the length of a full opera, in costume, with a live orchestra, in front of an audience.

Wight was building that in Jacksonville. The Ernest Stone Performing Arts Center gave him a real venue. What he built inside it came from the work he required.

---

## The Productions

*Pirates of Penzance* — Gilbert and Sullivan, the comedic one, where the Major-General sings faster than seems humanly possible. It requires singers who can land comedy while maintaining operatic technique. It is harder than it looks.

*Hansel and Gretel* — Humperdinck's opera, not the cartoon. One of the most beautiful scores in the German repertoire. The backstage photograph from opening night 2007 is the kind of image that catches what it felt like: cast in costume, flowers, the bulletin board behind them with room numbers for the music building.

*The Mercato* — performed at Ernest Stone PAC and Pell City Center. Full production, still real.

---

## The JSU Music Community

The students in these productions were part of a specific music community at JSU in the mid-2000s. The friendships made in those productions were the kind you make when working on something serious together, in close quarters, under real pressure.

Jean Ellison was in that community. So were others who stayed in music, in the region, and in each other's lives for years afterward.

The Anniston Museum of Natural History. The piano. The formal dress. The performance in a space not built for opera, making it fit anyway. That is what Wight's students were doing by 2008 — carrying what they'd learned into every room they walked into.
