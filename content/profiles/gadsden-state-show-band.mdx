My friend Kristian Crowe got me into Gadsden State. We went to high school together, and he knew they had a show band, and he knew they were looking for singers, and somewhere in that chain of knowing, I ended up with a full scholarship to sing with them.

The scholarship covered tuition. What it gave me beyond that is harder to account for.

---

## The Repertoire

The Gadsden State Show Band did Nat King Cole. Frank Sinatra. The Tennessee Waltz. This is not what you might expect from a show band attached to a community college in northeast Alabama, but it is what they did, and they did it well.

I sang those songs in front of audiences who had grown up with them — who remembered when those songs were new, when they played on the radio and meant something specific about a particular Saturday night. There is a different kind of attention in a room like that. People are not watching to be entertained. They are watching because something is being returned to them.

I was twenty, maybe. I did not entirely understand what I was participating in. I understand it better now.

---

## The Mayor's Mansion

One year, we performed at the Governor's mansion in Montgomery. Or possibly the Mayor's mansion — memory being what memory is. What I know for certain is that it was someone's mansion, it was official, and we were on our best behavior for the performance.

I danced with the wife of whoever owned the house. She was gracious about it. I tried to be gracious back. The music was good.

After the performance, the bus was loading. Someone — and I will not specify who, though the record should reflect that it was the kind of decision that seems obvious in the moment — jumped off the bus, crossed the yard, and went into the pool.

The pool was not for guests.

I jumped in with my shorts on. The shorts did not stay on. My boxer shorts did not stay on either.

What I can tell you is that the water was warm and the sky was dark and none of this showed up on a police report.

---

## On the Bus

On the way back, someone put on Southern Culture on the Skids. "Banana Pudding." If you know the song, you understand why that felt exactly right.

My friend Mario Richardson-Ray and I would dance on that bus. He had grown up with a completely different set of musical languages than I had — stomp clapping, particular rhythms, ways of moving that I had never been taught. He taught me. We would perform in high schools across the area, and when we got loose like that, the kids would go absolutely wild.

That is what music is for when it is working right.

---

## What Came After

Gadsden State was where I met Heather — at the Madrigal Dinner, in the second year, when I was cast as the Jester. She was watching. I was in costume doing my absolute best. Something started there.

It was also where I met Nick Trammell, whose father Mark Trammell sang baritone for Gold City, one of the great Southern Gospel quartets. Nick and Justin Bearden and Brandon and I started a group called Narrow Way. We sang in churches all over northeast Alabama. I have recordings somewhere. I think.

The scholarship was the door. What came through it was something else entirely.
