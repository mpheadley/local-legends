There are people who show up at more than one place in your story. Dr. Patricia Corbin showed up at several.

She was the Director of Choral Activities at Gadsden State — organized the choir, ran the Madrigal Dinner, recruited students and shaped them. She was the organist and choir director at Saint Michael and All Angels Episcopal Church in Anniston. She gave me a full vocal scholarship. She presided at Jason Wright's service.

And she died, and it was hard, and I was not able to join the choir when they sang for her.

---

## The Scholarship

I sang under Dr. Corbin at Gadsden State on a full vocal scholarship. My friend Kristian Crowe from high school knew they were looking for singers and helped make the connection. The scholarship covered tuition.

It also meant I was expected to show up, learn the parts, and perform with the choir, the Show Band, and whatever else was on the calendar. Dr. Corbin was not especially interested in excuses. She expected preparation. She expected you to take the music seriously, which meant taking the audience seriously, which meant treating your own voice not as a gift you'd been handed but as something you had to keep earning.

---

## The Madrigal Dinner

Every year she organized the Madrigal Dinner. Students played roles — courtiers, a jester, the whole Renaissance court. The audience came in costume sometimes. The food was served ceremonially. The music was live and continuous and genuinely festive in the way that things are festive when everyone plays it straight.

My second year, I was cast as the Jester.

That is the year I started dating Heather. She was watching. I was in full costume, performing in front of a room full of people, doing my absolute best at a role that required you to be loud and ridiculous on purpose. Whatever she saw that night, she saw enough to want to keep talking.

We have been married a long time now. I still think about the costume.

---

## The Golf Cart

I borrowed a golf cart from the GSU lot. More accurately: I and my friend Codi Harrell took a golf cart without asking anyone and did donuts in a parking lot until we got caught.

Dr. Corbin found out. I do not remember the exact conversation. I remember its texture — the specific silence of someone who expected better from you and is making sure you understand why.

---

## The Florida Trip

At some point, her mother passed away in Florida. She brought a choir to sing at the service — a Unitarian Universalist church, not the kind of place most of us had spent much time in. But a service is a service. Music is what you bring when words aren't enough.

A group of us drove down.

On the interstate — Georgia or Florida, the exact spot is gone now — one of my friends had to stop. He had irritable bowel syndrome, and the road had done what road food does to someone with IBS, and he didn't make it to a rest stop. He ran off the interstate into the trees.

The rest of us waited in the van. "Everybody Hurts" came on — the Michael Stipe one, slow and unambiguous about pain — and something said *go check on him.*

I got out and started across the median.

An 18-wheeler got very close to me. Real truck, highway speed, me in the road.

I survived. My friend survived. We made it to Florida. Dr. Corbin's choir sang her mother home in a Unitarian Universalist church, and it was beautiful.

---

## Saint Michael and All Angels

She was the organist and choir director at Saint Michael and All Angels Episcopal Church in Anniston. I did not know that part of her life the way I knew her in the classroom and on stage. But I knew she was there, and that her presence in that building meant something to the people who came there.

When she died, they placed her ashes on the grounds of the church. Jeremy Benson played flute. I was not able to join the choir when they sang for her, and I have thought about that more than once since.

She also presided at Jason Wright's service. She was the kind of person who showed up for those moments — who understood that what you do at the end is as important as what you do in the middle.

---

## What I Remember Most

She built things out of students who had jobs and drove long distances and learned their parts in apartments and dorm rooms. Choral music at a community college is not glamorous in the way people imagine music to be glamorous. It is real, which is harder.

I was young and not always reliable. She kept working with me anyway.
