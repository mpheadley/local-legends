Lori Floyd goes into the woods.

She crawls under fences. She brings kids who have run out of other options and takes them somewhere the walls don't follow — the trees, the trail, the particular quiet of a forest that does not care about your diagnosis or your case file.

She directs The Right Place, a program in Anniston that uses nature as therapy. Not metaphorically. Literally: outside, in the landscape, doing the physical work of being in a place that is not a school or a clinic or a home that isn't working.

---

## What The Right Place Does

Nature-based programming for youth. The kind of work that is easy to describe in a sentence and very hard to do.

The kids who come to The Right Place have often run out of other options. The school has tried. The clinic has tried. The parents are doing what they can. And then someone says: try this. Go outside. Get your hands dirty. Climb something.

That is the intervention, and it turns out to work more often than the clinical model expects.

Lori Floyd is the person who decided this was worth building in Anniston, Alabama, and then built it. The city has the woods for it — the ridge lines, the creek bottoms, the state park acreage nearby. What it needed was someone willing to lead the way in.

---

## The Director

She is the kind of director who goes first. She doesn't send the kids ahead while she manages logistics. She goes into the woods.

That matters. The kids notice. They know the difference between someone who believes the thing they're asking you to do and someone who is administering a program.

Lori Floyd believes it.
