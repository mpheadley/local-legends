The last person to buy flowers from the Headley Flower Farm was Tammy Jackson.

It was August 2025. The farm was weeks from closing. Tammy ordered through Heather. The flowers were for her first granddaughter's baptism.

[MATT: Confirm this with Heather. Confirm the month. Do not include the granddaughter's name or the date of the baptism.]

I did not know it at the time. I was in a psychiatric center at UAB that October. She texted me in November, after I got out, to say she had been praying for me. She said she didn't want to intrude. She said, and this is exact: "I miss seeing you. And that's not meant to be pressure to come to church. Just acknowledgment that you are cared about and made valuable contributions to our team."

When I eventually told her about the bipolar diagnosis, that I had been manic when I resigned on Ash Wednesday, she wrote back:

"My concern has always been your health and wellbeing. The job was entirely secondary."

She offered her apology again. She had already apologized once before. She offered it again.

"The apology is gratefully received but unneeded."

That is a sentence that takes a long time to write. She has probably spent a long time earning the ability to say it.

---

The Ash Wednesday confrontation is in the record. March 5, 2025. I missed a planning call. I was working from home, navigating family health challenges, trying to keep things together. She texted: "I don't feel like you've handled today well. Jason and I will handle it. Stay home."

She was right that I had not handled things well. What she did not have, and what neither of us had, was a name for why.

The paranoid ideation came later that month. I will not put in writing what I believed about Tammy during the worst of the episode. It was clinical. It was the manic state in documentary form. None of it was true.

She managed a situation she was not equipped for because there was no tool that would have equipped her. She did not have the language for what she was seeing. I did not have the language for what I was living. We were both doing our best in the dark.

---

When she bought the baptism flowers, she did not know the farm was closing. She bought them because her granddaughter was being baptized and she wanted flowers.

That is all.

But the loop it closes is something I have been sitting with for a long time. The pastor who handled the hardest year at the church where I worked, who processed the resignation through the SPRC, who sent the letter, who texted in November with genuine warmth, who received my apology without needing it and offered her own again — she is the one who bought the last flowers.

Not a metaphor. Just what happened.
