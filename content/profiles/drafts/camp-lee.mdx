Sage and Soren were baptized there. On Pentecost. In front of both congregations.

May 15, 2016. We drove both churches out to Camp Lee. Lincoln and Weaver, two United Methodist congregations in two counties, combined for one Sunday at the water's edge. Sherill Clontz came. Our district superintendent. She preached, and she led the baptisms. Ellis Lazarre was baptized that day too.

I stood there with Heather. The twins were infants. Sherill took them one at a time. The lake was right behind her. The whole thing was water and words and the smell of Alabama in May.

After lunch, the kids did the zipline. People canoed on the lake. It was a good day to be the church.

I'd been going to Camp Lee for years before that. Worship retreats, leadership weekends, the annual summer camp my churches sent kids to. The Lakeview building. The cafeteria. The obstacle course. The pool.

In July 2023, I ran the worship for junior high residential camp. I made a setlist. Handchimes, egg shakers, drums. "Graves into Gardens." "Build Your Kingdom Here." Kids from half a dozen churches, crammed into bunkhouses, singing like the world depended on it. We had a sunrise service. I wrote the devo the night before.

I don't know how to explain what it feels like to lead worship at a place like that. There's something about the humidity of it. The way sound carries over water. You're outdoors and the world has shrunk to twenty teenagers and a lake and a God who might or might not be listening, and somehow it doesn't matter because they're all still singing.

---

In the summer of 2024, I wrote about depression in *The Anniston Star*. About sitting by the Camp Lee pool surrounded by people, church people, friends, families, and feeling completely alone. About the way you can be visible and invisible at the same time. About hiking into the woods with my anger because I didn't know what else to do with it.

I don't think I was looking for anything out there.

Maybe that was the point.

---

The fall after I got out of the hospital, I worked at Camp Lee. Manual labor. They were building an obstacle course, platforms for ropes around the property, and I was one of the people picking up stones, moving them, blowing leaves, making it happen.

I wanted to do something with my hands. I also wanted to be somewhere that might eventually lead back to something. A job. A role. Some version of what I'd had before.

One afternoon, the church staff came out to test the obstacle course. They walked past me. I tried to make it not awkward. There was an office on that property, in the building, that I had once expected to occupy. Someone else was using it now.

Nobody said anything about it. The geography said it.

I moved another rock.

---

I drive past it on my way to other things. Seventy Camp Lee Main Road. The sign is still there. The lake is still there.

Sage and Soren are ten now. They don't remember the baptism. They weren't old enough. But I was there, and Sherill was there, and both congregations were there, and it was Pentecost, and the water was cold.

That happened here. Whatever else did.
