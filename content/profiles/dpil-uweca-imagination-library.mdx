Dolly Parton's father couldn't read. He was a brilliant man — built his own house, raised twelve children in the mountains of East Tennessee — but he never learned to read. She watched him sign his name with an X.

In 1995, she started the Imagination Library in Sevier County, the county where she grew up. The idea was simple: every child who enrolled would receive one free, age-appropriate book in the mail every month, from birth until the month they turned five. Their name on the envelope. Their book.

By 2003, the program had mailed one million books. By 2018, Parton stood in the Library of Congress to commemorate the hundred-millionth. In 2023, the two-hundred-millionth. Today the program reaches more than three million children every month — in seven countries — and has now gifted over 300 million books worldwide. In the United States, one in seven children under five receives a book in the mail each month from the Imagination Library.

Research backs what parents already know: children who receive books early read more, come to school more prepared, and develop a relationship with reading that carries. A 2023 study — the largest shared-book-reading study ever conducted — found that Dolly Parton's Imagination Library dramatically improves early literacy outcomes. Parents report their children are more interested in books. They report feeling more comfortable reading aloud. The books arrive. The habit builds.

---

In Calhoun, Cleburne, and Randolph Counties, the program runs through United Way of East Central Alabama.

[Shannon Jenkins](/profiles/shannon-jenkins), who has led UWECA for over twenty-two years, oversees the administration locally. Last year alone, UWECA mailed more than 40,000 books to children in the three-county area. The program is free to families — enrollment takes five minutes. Any child living in Calhoun, Cleburne, or Randolph County, from birth to age five, qualifies.

The cost to the program is about two dollars per book per child per month. It doesn't sound like much. Multiply it across a year, across thousands of children, and the funding work becomes real. UWECA carries it — through United Way's annual campaign, through community donors, through people who understand that the investment in a child at two pays back for decades.

On October 18, 2026, United Way of East Central Alabama will be on the floor of The Aisle Bridal Expo at Anniston Museums & Gardens.

Most of the people walking through that door are planning a wedding. Many of them are also planning a family. The Imagination Library doesn't require you to wait. You can enroll a child who isn't born yet. You can enroll a niece, a neighbor's baby, the child of a friend who's never heard of the program.

The program is free to families. If you want to give so another family can participate, you can do that too.
