Johnny Ponder grew up in Munford at the foot of Cheaha Mountain.

He writes about it now — short essays for Alabama Pioneers, a site dedicated to preserving living Alabama memory. The titles give you the outline: "Growing up in Munford, Alabama in the 1950s and 1960s." "Why don't you look behind you? — A Simple Lesson to Learn from a Grandfather." Boys on bikes. Fishing creeks. Arrowheads in plowed fields. BB guns and bows made from creek-side saplings. A boyhood that lasted maybe twenty years in Alabama's particular geography before it was gone.

He went into the woods professionally. Forty years as a forester.

He came home.

---

## What He Built When He Got Back

Johnny Ponder joined the Talladega County School Board in November 1994, District 3 — the district that includes Munford, where he went to school himself. He has been there since.

In that time, he was integral to developing something that Munford schools have that almost no other public school system in Alabama has: a 130-acre outdoor education campus at the base of Cheaha Mountain, adjacent to the Talladega National Forest.

The campus includes twelve outdoor classrooms, an amphitheater, a nature trail, a longleaf pine stand, a low ropes course, a greenhouse, a fish pond, a frog pond, a five-tier wetland, butterfly gardens, a bluebird trail, and an aquaculture facility. Inside the school buildings: a lobby designed to look like a cave. Hallways with life-sized tree replicas. A cafeteria with murals of the Talladega National Forest. A 250-gallon aquarium.

The Alabama Farmers Federation featured Munford schools as a model for outdoor education tied to forestry. Georgia-Pacific and the Forest Workforce Training Institute donated materials. The program drew statewide notice because it was working — kids in Munford were learning biology, ecology, and natural systems by walking into the forest that had been there since before the county was founded.

Ponder's own description of what they were trying to do: "We're just trying to encourage kids to get off their laptops and phones and do some stuff outside."

---

## The Long Logic

The through line of Johnny Ponder's life is not difficult to find. It is the forest.

He grew up in it — the woods at the foot of Cheaha, the same Talladega National Forest that now borders the school campus he helped build. He went into it professionally for forty years. He came home and built a school system around it, so the children of Munford could learn what he learned in the same landscape where he learned it.

The 130-acre outdoor classroom is not a program a consultant proposed. It is the work of a man who knew what the forest had given him and decided that was something worth passing on.

---

## What He Writes

In retirement, Ponder writes for Alabama Pioneers. The pieces are short, specific, and plain. They are about a particular neighborhood, particular boys, a particular decade in a particular Alabama town. They do not explain themselves. They do not argue for anything. They just say what it was.

That is its own form of preservation — the long memory of a place, kept by someone who was there, written down before the last person who remembers is gone.
