The music program at Gadsden State doesn't happen by accident. It takes someone at the top of the department who believes that a community college in northeast Alabama should have a real music program — not a music appreciation elective, not a general arts requirement, but an actual living department with a show band, a choral program, faculty who have careers, and students who come because they can actually develop as musicians.

Dr. Renée Baptiste was that person. She headed the David L. Walters Music Department at Gadsden State Community College. The department takes its name from David L. Walters, and inside it was a collection of faculty and programs — Dr. Patricia Corbin's choral work, the Gadsden State Show Band, the whole structure that gave a kid from a NE Alabama high school a reason to come and a scholarship to come on.

---

## What a Department Head Does

Most of what a department chair does is invisible. The budget negotiations, the hiring decisions, the accreditation work, the conversations with administration about why music matters and what it costs. Students experience the results — the faculty, the performance spaces, the programs — without seeing the infrastructure behind them.

Dr. Baptiste maintained the infrastructure. In a community college setting, that is not a small thing. Community colleges exist under real resource pressure. Music departments are often the first thing cut. That the Gadsden State music program was what it was — the David L. Walters building, real faculty with real credentials, a show band good enough to perform at the Mayor's mansion — is evidence that someone was fighting for it at the level where those fights happen.

---

## Northeast Alabama Music in That Period

The years when Dr. Baptiste led the department were the same years when the Gadsden State music program was producing people who then went on to perform, teach, compose, and build music careers across the region and beyond.

This is not coincidence. These things track. A department head who believes in the work creates conditions for the work to happen. The specific names — Dr. Corbin and the Madrigal Dinner, the Show Band and its repertoire, the scholarships that brought students in who couldn't have come otherwise — those are the visible results. Dr. Baptiste is the institutional fact behind them.
