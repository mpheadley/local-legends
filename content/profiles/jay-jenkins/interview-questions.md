Questions

- 1. The Caldwell Building — Your father Julian was involved in preserving it. What do you remember about watching that fight? Did that shape how you think about buildings?

- 2. Little River Canyon Center — 25,000 sq ft on the edge of the most dramatic landscape in Alabama. What was the hardest design constraint — and what did the canyon teach you?

- 3. The baseball field in Oxford — Whose idea was it, and when you see kids using that field now, what do you think about?

- 4. Martha's Hope pivot — You steered away from a $1.1M approach toward something that got built. Walk me through that decision.

- 5. West Anniston Master Plan — Cradle-to-career is a political phrase architects don't usually use. Who convinced you that was the right frame?

- 6. Denver years — You came back from Colorado to join your father in practice. What was that like — walking into practice alongside him?

- 7. AIA Alabama president, 2006 — Mostly invisible work. What did you actually try to change, and did it stick?

- 8. The RMC-UAB affiliation — You were board chair when it happened. What does a building tell you about an institution that its leadership can't?

- 9. Soccer coaching — Sixteen seasons. Why soccer?

- 10. The question I haven't asked — What's the thing Anniston still doesn't understand about itself that the buildings already know?

- BONUS — Your dad preserved a building from 1889. Is there a building in Anniston you'd quietly demolish if you could get away with it?

Key Things to Listen For

- The father thread — came back specifically to work WITH Julian, not just to come home

- Auburn: 7 years not 5 (1983–1990) — what happened in those extra years?

- Denver: the pull vs. the push. Why did he actually leave? Why come back?

- Martha's Hope: the specific moment the $2.9M plan broke down

- West Anniston: who put cradle-to-career on the table

- The resignation: personal turning point — what does that mean now?

- Soccer: 16 seasons + Donoho girls coach — something sustained that long has a reason

- A moment in a building he designed — standing somewhere, looking at something

Ethics Case — Know Before You Go In

This is public record. Dec 2018: voted to lease Anniston Star building as temp city hall — his wife worked in the Star's ad department in that building. Oct 2019: Ethics Commission ruled violation. Dec 2019: Indicted. March 2021: Pleaded guilty — 2 years probation, $250 fine. Stayed on council three more years. Oct 2024: Resigned citing personal turning point and health.

These are separate events 3 years apart. The GAP question (what were you naming when you said personal turning point) is the natural opening if you want to go there. You don't have to.
