There is a version of the music story where the talented kid leaves and does not come back. Gets the degree somewhere with a bigger name, books the roles, and the town that raised him only ever hears about it secondhand. That is the common version. It is not Eric Wilcox's.

Wilcox is an Alabama native who trained his tenor at Jacksonville State University, went to the University of New Mexico for a Master of Music in vocal performance, spent years on opera stages across the Southwest, and then did the thing the common version leaves out. He came home. Now he and his wife, Estefanía, run [Wilcox Music Studios](/profiles/estefania-cuevas-wilcox) in Oxford, and the training he went out and gathered is being handed to students in Calhoun County.

---

## The Roles

A tenor's résumé is a list of the characters he has become. Wilcox's list is a real one — the kind of roles a working opera singer earns and does not get handed.

Nemorino in *L'elisir d'amore*. Tamino in *The Magic Flute*. Romeo in *Roméo et Juliette*. Ferrando in *Così fan tutte*. Eisenstein in *Die Fledermaus*. In 2014 he was selected as a young apprentice for Opera Southwest's *Amleto*. He sang chorus with Opera Birmingham in *La Bohème* and *La Traviata* — the productions where you learn how a real company runs before you carry the lead in one.

He also did the other tenor work, the sacred and the recital kind: soloist in Handel's *Messiah*, Mozart's *Vesperae Solennes de Confessore*, the art song repertoire that a singer either respects or exposes himself trying to fake.

Along the way there were the markers that mean someone was paying attention. First place at the National Association of Teachers of Singing state and regional competitions. The 2012 Richard Armstrong Vocal Scholarship. A teaching assistantship in voice.

---

## What He Carries

What matters about a résumé like that is not the bragging. It is what it means for a student in Oxford.

When Wilcox teaches audition preparation, he is not describing an audition he read about. He walked into those rooms. When he coaches a high schooler toward a college scholarship audition or a graduate music program, he is coaching them toward doors he has already walked through. That is a different kind of teacher than the one who knows the theory. It is the one who has the map because he made the trip.

He has taught at Talladega College, the University of New Mexico, the Ars Nova School for the Arts, and the JSU Preparatory Program going back to 2009. Today he is an adjunct lecturer at Jacksonville State — private voice and Introduction to Music, out of Mason Hall — and a private instructor at The Donoho School.

His own teachers were Dr. Richard Armstrong, Dr. Ginger Beazley, Leslie Umphrey, and Dr. Nathan Wight. That lineage — the JSU voice faculty and the wider northeast Alabama music world that produced people like [Dr. Patricia Corbin](/profiles/dr-patricia-corbin) and [Blair Callaway](/profiles/blair-callaway) — is the water he came up in. He is now part of the reason it keeps running.

---

## The Return

The region has always produced more musicians than it gets credit for, and it has always lost too many of them to bigger cities that could pay. The story that keeps a place alive is the one where the person who trained goes out, gets good, and brings it back.

Eric Wilcox did that. The tenor who could have stayed gone is running lessons in Oxford, ages 11 and up, teaching kids in his own county how to make a room do what a room is supposed to do when it is sung to well.
