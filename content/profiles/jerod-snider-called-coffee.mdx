Jerod Snider is the kind of person who opens a coffee shop in a place that already has coffee and makes it matter anyway.

Jacksonville, Alabama has Jacksonville State University. JSU has students who need caffeine and a place to sit. That was already a known fact when Jerod opened Called Coffee. What he built was not a response to market data; it was a place with a specific point of view about what a coffee shop in a small Alabama town could be.

Called Coffee has a strong following. Not just among students — among the faculty who have been at JSU for twenty years, the locals who don't have JSU connections at all, and the people who live in Anniston or Oxford and make the drive to Jacksonville on a Saturday morning because the coffee is worth it and the room feels like somewhere.

---

## The New Porch

Jerod has a new porch. He talks about it the way a person who has spent time building community spaces talks about any good gathering spot: with the specificity that comes from knowing what makes people stay.

A porch is a threshold. You're not fully inside and not fully outside. The conversation is optional but available. People stay longer than they meant to.

That is what Called Coffee is. You go for the coffee. You stay for the hour you didn't know you had.

---

## Jacksonville

Jacksonville is not just a college town. That's worth saying because the college is what people from outside say first — it's the JSU town, the Gamecock town, the place with the stadium and the marching band.

But Jacksonville was a town before JSU arrived, and the people who live there year-round know a version of it that the student population cycles through and leaves. Jerod Snider is from that other Jacksonville. Called Coffee serves both versions.
