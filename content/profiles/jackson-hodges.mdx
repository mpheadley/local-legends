He graduated from The Donoho School in 2012 and stayed.

That is the thing about Anniston. Not everyone who grows up here stays. Jackson Hodges stayed, took a job in city government, and became the voice the city uses to explain itself to its own people.

He is the Director of Public Relations for the City of Anniston. He writes the releases. He runs the communications operation. He coordinates with the planning department and the mayor's office. In August 2023, the Alabama Chapter of the American Planning Association gave him and City Manager Steven Folks the Outstanding Media Coverage Award — for the way they talked about the city's work in public.

He also ran the census outreach with the Calhoun County Chamber. He helped stand up the Anniston City Youth Council. He sat on the homeless task force steering committee with United Way of East Central Alabama.

That is not a resume of someone waiting for the next thing. That is a person who decided to be useful where they were.

---

## Running for the County

In 2026, Jackson Hodges is running for the Calhoun County Commission, District 1.

His campaign platform has four words: blight, animals, litter, public safety. He calls them the "Four B's." He is thirty-two. It is his first race.

He does not appear to be running from something. He appears to be running toward it — toward the specific work of making a county livable. The strays on the roads. The abandoned houses. The trash in the right-of-ways. The unglamorous material work that someone has to want to do.

He grew up in a county that needed it. He is offering to do it.

*Jackson Hodges is the Director of Public Relations for the City of Anniston and a candidate for Calhoun County Commission, District 1. He is a graduate of The Donoho School (Class of 2012).*
