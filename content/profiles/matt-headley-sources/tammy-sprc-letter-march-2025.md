Subject: Reflections on My Experience Under Tammy's Leadership

Dear SPRC Members,

I am writing to provide a candid and professional account of my experiences working under Tammy's leadership at Anniston First UMC. My goal in sharing this is to ensure transparency, advocate for fair leadership practices, and communicate the circumstances that have led to my transition. I remain committed to the mission of the church and its congregation, and I want to move forward in a way that preserves my relationships within the church community while stepping into a role that aligns more fully with my calling.

**The Hiring Process and Initial Misrepresentation**

When I first saw the job posting for a Music Director position at Anniston First, I immediately felt it was a demotion and not the right fit for me. As someone with extensive pastoral experience, I knew I could bring much more to the table than just leading the music program. However, shortly after, Tammy directly reached out to me about an Associate Pastor role, which she framed as my "dream job."

Tammy assured me that this Associate Pastor position would allow me to maintain my pastoral status and duties, while also overseeing the music program. This sounded like the perfect opportunity—I would get to continue in ministry while also utilizing my musical talents. I did express initial concerns about whether the church could truly sustain four pastors, but Tammy assured me that she believed it was possible.

However, when the actual job offer was put in front of me, the responsibilities were very different from what Tammy had described. It felt like a clear "bait and switch." The role I was offered bore little resemblance to the one Tammy had sold me on. Rather than the autonomy and respect I expected as a pastor, I was effectively placed in a glorified Music Director role with far less authority and compensation than what had been originally presented. This created an immediate imbalance in expectations versus reality.

**Consistent Undervaluing and Lack of Institutional Support**

Over the course of my four years in the Associate Pastor role, I repeatedly felt undervalued, disrespected, and misled by Tammy. It became evident that she had acted unilaterally in hiring me, without obtaining the proper approval or support of the SPRC as required by church policy.

Unlike the other pastors, I was not afforded the same benefits, pastoral authority, or institutional recognition, despite consistently receiving positive feedback from the congregation. When I confronted Tammy about feeling misled, she responded in a way that seemed dismissive and insincere. The broader church system, by allowing this imbalance to persist, also played a role in devaluing my contributions.

**Repeated Denial of Fair Compensation & Professional Growth**

Throughout my time at Anniston First, I made repeated requests for fair compensation adjustments, including a cost-of-living increase. These requests were consistently denied by Tammy and church leadership, despite my belief that my contributions far exceeded what I was being compensated for.

While I was told that the budget did not allow for salary adjustments, I witnessed financial decisions that indicated otherwise. The message I received—both explicitly and implicitly—was that my role was not valued at the same level as others, despite my extensive contributions.

**The Resignation Decision & Tammy's Unilateral Actions**

Ultimately, the consistent lack of support, recognition, and fair treatment from Tammy and church leadership led to my decision to resign. I no longer felt I could thrive in an environment where my contributions were systematically undervalued, and my concerns were dismissed rather than addressed.

Tammy's handling of my resignation further reinforced these issues. She acted unilaterally in decisions related to my departure, without SPRC involvement or proper approval. This further demonstrated the same patterns of control, manipulation, and lack of transparency that I had experienced throughout my tenure.

**Tammy's Leadership Patterns & Their Impact**

Based on my experience, I have identified several leadership behaviors from Tammy that I believe warrant attention:

- Avoidance & Passive Leadership (Unhealthy Enneagram 9 Traits in Stress)
- Manipulative & Image-Focused Leadership (Unhealthy Enneagram 3 Traits)
- Lack of Institutional Integrity & Professionalism

**My Goals in Sharing This With SPRC**

I am not sharing this to cause division or attack Tammy personally, but rather to advocate for fair treatment and transparency in leadership.

I also recognize that my experiences are not unique, and my concerns reflect larger leadership patterns that could affect future staff and the health of the church overall. My hope is that by sharing my experiences, SPRC can take a more active role in ensuring fairness, accountability, and institutional integrity moving forward.

As I step into a new phase of ministry—one that aligns more with my calling—I want to maintain my relationships with the people of Anniston First. I deeply value the church community, and I remain open to collaboration in ways that do not place me under the same harmful leadership dynamics.

I appreciate your time and consideration in reviewing my experience, and I am available for further conversation if needed.

Grace and peace,
Matt Headley
