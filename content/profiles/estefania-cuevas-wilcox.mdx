Some people in this region were born here and stayed. Some left and came back. Estefanía Cuevas Wilcox belongs to the third and rarer category: the person who could have built her life anywhere and chose Alabama on purpose.

She is a soprano who made her professional debut with the New Mexico Philharmonic in 2016. She trained at Jacksonville State University — a Bachelor of Arts in music with a minor in drama — and at the University of New Mexico, where she took a Master of Music in vocal performance with an emphasis in musicology. And she is now one half of [Wilcox Music Studios](/profiles/eric-wilcox) in Oxford, teaching voice and piano to students who range from four-year-olds at the keyboard to teenagers preparing for college auditions.

---

## The Roles

Her repertoire is the soprano's inheritance — the parts written for a voice that has to be both agile and enormous, sometimes in the same aria.

Rosalinda in *Die Fledermaus*. Pamina in *The Magic Flute*. Juliette in *Roméo et Juliette*. Donna Anna in *Don Giovanni* — a role that separates sopranos who can sing from sopranos who can act while they sing, because Donna Anna is grief and fury from her first note.

The recognition came early. She was selected as a scholarship winner through the Birmingham Music Club Guild Voice Competition in 2007 and again in 2009, and placed at the NATS state and regional level. Her teachers were [Dr. Patricia Corbin](/profiles/dr-patricia-corbin), Dr. Ginger Beazley, Dr. Wendy Faughn, Dr. Michael Hix, and Dr. Gail Steward — the JSU and Southwest voice faculties that shaped a generation of singers.

That she studied under Dr. Corbin matters here. Corbin built the JSU opera program that gave northeast Alabama a serious classical vocal tradition. Estefanía is one of the singers that program sent into the world — and one of the few who came back to teach in the same county.

---

## Voice and Piano, Ages 4 and Up

The thing about the Wilcox studio is that it does not only train the eleven-year-old who already wants to be a singer. Estefanía teaches piano starting at age four — the age where music is still mostly play, before technique, before recitals, when the only job is to make the instrument feel like a friend instead of a chore.

She has been running private studio instruction since 2006, and has taught at The Donoho School, the Ars Nova School of the Arts, and the Gadsden Community School for the Arts. She lectured at Jacksonville State in 2017. She is, in other words, not a performer who dabbles in teaching. She is a teacher who happens to be able to sing Donna Anna.

---

## The Choice

The diaspora story usually runs one direction: talented people leave Alabama for the coasts and the cities and the philharmonics. Estefanía ran it the other way. She had the New Mexico Philharmonic on her résumé and she planted a music studio in Oxford, where a kid whose family could never afford a conservatory can sit down at a piano with someone who has actually stood on the professional stage.

That is not a consolation prize. That is the whole point. The best thing a region can have is not the musicians who got out. It is the ones who came, or came back, and stayed to teach.
