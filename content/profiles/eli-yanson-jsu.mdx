Dr. Eliezer Yanson Jr. was born and raised in the Philippines.

He came to the United States to study at Bob Jones University in Greenville, South Carolina — a path that says something about the particular geography of evangelical music education, the places that have trained the best choral conductors without always getting the recognition for it. He earned his bachelor's in piano performance and his master's in church music (conducting) at BJU. His Doctor of Musical Arts in choral conducting came from the University of South Carolina.

He then spent nine years on the music faculty at Bob Jones University before moving to Alabama.

He has been at Jacksonville State University ever since.

---

## What He Does Here

At JSU, Yanson conducts the A Cappella Choir, the Chamber Singers, and the Civic Chorale. He teaches undergraduate and graduate music classes. His department is the David L. Walters Department of Music — one of those institutions that exists inside a regional university in a small Alabama town, doing work that goes largely unseen by anyone who doesn't already know to look for it.

He is an associate professor of music. His conducting mentors were Larry Wyatt and Warren Cook.

---

## Carnegie Hall

In 2015, Yanson took the JSU A Cappella Choir to Carnegie Hall.

In 2018, he took them back.

In 2023, he took them a third time.

Each time, the choir placed in the top tier of its division in the American Prize — a national choral competition. In 2015 and 2023, they won third prize in the College/University Chorus category.

For context: the American Prize is not a regional competition. It is a national assessment of choral quality, adjudicated by professional musicians from across the country. A university choir from a mid-sized institution in Calhoun County, Alabama, winning third place nationally — and doing it in 2015, in 2018, and again in 2023 — is not an accident of scheduling. It is the result of a particular conductor, building a particular culture, year after year, in a particular place.

---

## The World Outside Alabama

Yanson has conducted choirs and led clinics in Thailand, Bulgaria, and the Philippines. He serves on the faculty of the Varna International Music Academy in Bulgaria — which means that a conductor based in Jacksonville, Alabama, is spending part of his calendar working in the Black Sea resort city of Varna, teaching advanced choral technique to musicians from across Europe.

This is what it looks like when talent stays rather than leaves. It does not mean the talent is local in origin — Yanson came from the Philippines by way of South Carolina. But he arrived in Jacksonville and stayed, and in staying, built something that has now traveled from this county to Carnegie Hall three times and keeps going back.

---

## The Inverse Diaspora

Much of what Southern Legends covers is the story of people who left and what they carried with them.

Eli Yanson is the other version of that story. He came from elsewhere — from Mindanao and Greenville and Columbia — and arrived in a small Alabama city and chose to plant a music program that has now become one of the better choral programs in the country. He chose Jacksonville. That choice is what made Carnegie Hall possible three times.

What NE Alabama looks like to someone who came here from the Philippines by way of Bob Jones University is not a question I can answer for him. But the fact that he's here, and that the choir keeps going to New York, suggests the answer involves more than inertia.
