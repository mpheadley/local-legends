Matt Mitchell is an 8th-generation Alabamian from Walker County.

Walker County is coal country, the foothills of the Appalachians above Birmingham — the kind of place that makes a particular kind of Alabamian, one who knows what it is to live in the part of the state that the rest of the country has already decided about. Matt Mitchell grew up there, and he made it funny, and then he made it very, very big.

He goes by @alostrich online. The name is appropriately odd for someone whose comedy has always lived at the edge of Southern identity and self-awareness.

---

## It's a Southern Thing

Mitchell joined "It's a Southern Thing" — the viral sketch comedy channel — within a month of its launch. He spent nearly five years there as a writer and on-camera talent. The channel became one of the most widely shared regional comedy brands in the country, accumulating more than two billion video views on Facebook and YouTube.

In 2021, his sketch "If Genies Were Southern" won the People's Choice Webby Award for Shortform Comedy Video. He received two Emmy nominations for writing and hosting work during his time with the channel. These are not regional awards. These are recognitions that came from the national entertainment industry finding something real in what he was doing.

He was making content about the South from inside the South, without apology or irony, and people were watching — not just in Alabama, not just in the Southeast, but everywhere.

---

## The Departure

In 2022, Mitchell left "It's a Southern Thing."

He was quiet about it for a while. In February 2023, he spoke publicly. The departure was not entirely on his own terms, and what followed it was frustrating: the channel kept using his videos, his image, images of his family, and making money from them after he was gone. "It's not been great," he said, "and very frustrating that they won't sever those ties and they are still making money, and I am not getting any of it."

This is the part of the viral content economy that doesn't get discussed enough: the people who built the thing don't always own it. The South that made him famous was in the brand, but the brand was not his.

---

## What He's Building Now

Since leaving, Mitchell has been building his independent audience. He hosts trivia and music trivia nights in Jasper and Cullman. He keeps his own social channels active. He is figuring out what it looks like to make a living at this without the corporate infrastructure, which is the harder version of the problem.

The Walker County Arts Alliance includes him among their recognized regional artists. His website is alostrich.com.

He is, by most measures, at the beginning of the second chapter — the part after the big machine, where the work becomes more his and the audience has to catch up with where he actually is.

---

## What He Represents

There are a lot of people who make content about the South for an outside audience — packaging it, explaining it, making it palatable. Matt Mitchell was never doing that. He was making content from inside the thing, for people who recognized it, and it turned out that recognition was portable across geography in ways that no one quite predicted.

The departure and its aftermath are also a Southern story in their own right: the creative worker who built something valuable for someone else and came out the other end with the skill but not the asset. He is still building. Eight generations of Walker County gives you that much stubbornness at minimum.
