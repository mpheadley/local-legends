He grew up in Greenville, Alabama — a small town in the Black Belt, Butler County — and his father left when he was seven years old.

He has said the wound pointed him toward the weather. An abandoned child in a town where the sky could change everything in under a minute. He found something in storms that neither confused nor abandoned him. They were awful and true and you could watch them coming if you knew how to look.

He learned how to look.

---

## Forty-Seven Years in the Alabama Sky

James Spann has been a broadcast meteorologist in Alabama for forty-seven years. He was Chief Meteorologist at ABC 33/40 in Birmingham, one of the most respected weather voices in the South. In 2025 he launched the **Alabama Weather Network**, a streaming service built for the job he has always wanted — wall-to-wall severe weather coverage, statewide, no interruptions for commercials when the sky is falling.

He is a Certified Broadcast Meteorologist. He has won nine AP awards for best weather anchor in Alabama. Multiple Emmys. The National Broadcaster of the Year. The FLASH National Weather Person of the Year. He wrote *Weathering Life*, a memoir where faith and the atmosphere share the same chapter.

His motto: *"Every job is a ministry."*

He means that literally.

---

## The Ministry He Runs on Sundays

James Spann has been a member of Double Oak Community Church in Shelby County for years. He leads children's worship every week. He participates in vacation Bible school. He preaches to kids.

He is not performing two separate lives. The storm coverage and the children's ministry are the same life looked at from different angles. A man who survived abandonment by learning to watch the sky. A man who found a church and chose to serve the youngest people in the room.

<PullQuote>
Every job is a ministry.
</PullQuote>

## Northeast Alabama

On March 25, 2021, a tornado outbreak struck Calhoun County. Five people were killed. James Spann was in his studio for all of it — running continuous coverage while, as later reported, his own home in Shelby County was being damaged by the same system.

He stayed in the chair.

That is what forty-seven years of "every job is a ministry" looks like. The sky is going to do what the sky does. Your job is to be in the room when it does, and to tell people the truth about what is coming so they have time to move.

He has never missed a storm.
