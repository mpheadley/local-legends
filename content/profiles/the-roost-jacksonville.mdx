Jacksonville is a small city that has more going on than it gets credit for. It sits at the top of Calhoun County, just southeast of JSU's campus, close enough to the Cleburne County line that you can feel the foothills starting to lift the terrain. The Chief Ladiga Trail cuts through, which has brought a different kind of traveler to the area — the sort of person who wants to be in a place, not just pass through it. Devan Sonderman has built his properties around that same instinct.

The Roost is a wedding and event venue that carries the character of the community it's in. It doesn't pretend to be something other than what it is, which is part of the appeal. Good venues in small cities tend to work this way: they've absorbed the story of the place, and that story becomes part of the experience for the couple and their guests. A wedding at The Roost in Jacksonville tells a different story than a wedding in a generic ballroom, and that difference is what couples are usually reaching for when they say they want something "unique." The Chief Ladiga Trail, which runs forty-some miles between Anniston and Cleburne County, has brought cyclists and hikers to this corridor and generated a particular kind of trail-town culture: unpretentious, active, locally rooted. Devan's properties fit that culture. They're built for people who want the experience to feel real rather than produced.
