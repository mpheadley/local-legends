# A New Way of Growing
**Aquality Farms — Anniston, Alabama**

*Submitted to Foothills Magazine by Matt Headley*
*Matt Headley is a former pastor, flower farmer, and classically trained singer from Northeast Alabama. He writes profiles of local people and businesses at [southernlegends.blog](https://southernlegends.blog) and builds websites for small businesses at [headleyweb.com](https://headleyweb.com).*

---

On Saturday mornings at the Downtown Anniston Farmers Market — and now at markets across the region — Samuel Sawyer hauls in two big white coolers and sets up a booth that always draws a crowd. Mushrooms, lettuce, tinctures, and a juice he offers in small cups to anyone passing by. People stop. Then they stay. He has a way of pulling out a mushroom, explaining how it grew, and making you feel like you've been missing something. John Mark takes the next person in line before Samuel even finishes. They never rush anybody.

What the Sawyer brothers have built from those Saturday conversations is one of the more remarkable things happening in downtown Anniston right now — 24,000 heads of lettuce and 300 pounds of gourmet mushrooms a week, grown in a 6,000-square-foot converted warehouse on Wilmer Avenue. No soil. No pesticides. No acreage. Just vertical towers, hydroponic science, and a family building.

The building at 1414 Wilmer Avenue has been in the Sawyer family for thirty years. Their grandparents ran Sawyer Anniston Pharmacy from the late sixties until 1999. Their father Mark ran produce out of the same space. When Samuel and John Mark decided to bring it back to life, they kept the family mission and changed the method. Samuel says the whole thing started with notes on a napkin — but that napkin had five years of research behind it. They spent six years studying hydroponics before they touched a wall. The storefront opened in April 2025.

Inside, the front 2,000 square feet grows lettuce and microgreens in six-week cycles under colored grow lights. The back 1,000 square feet is the mushroom lab — Lion's Mane, Oyster, Chestnut, Reishi, each species with its own science. Samuel knows all of it and will walk you through it if you have ten minutes.

The farm's reach extends well past Wilmer Avenue. Aquality Farms partners with the Alabama Farm to School Program, supplying pesticide-free produce to over 35,000 students across the region. A School Sponsorship Program, launched this year, lets local businesses fund produce for students directly. The storefront sells fresh produce, herbs, and mushroom tinctures to the public, alongside a Harvest Box built with other local farms and businesses.

When asked what drives it all, Samuel doesn't hesitate: "The overarching theme in what we do is centered around health. Health for the individual, community, and the environment. This is much more than a business — it gives people access to natural wellness products, a different perspective, and more importantly a community that listens and supports."

He also runs Chaha Outdoors, an outdoor wellness initiative named after the Creek word for "high place" — the same root as Cheaha Mountain, thirty minutes away. Wellness walks, trail runs, a community learning the county's trails. The farm grows food that's good for you. Chaha gets you outside. Same mission, different door.

Every business that opens in downtown Anniston is a bet on the revitalization. Samuel and John Mark made that bet with a warehouse, some vertical towers, and a mushroom lab. In pounds of produce per week, they're winning.
