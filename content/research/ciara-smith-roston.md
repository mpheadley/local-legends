## Personal Connection (Matt + Heather)

Visited Headley Flower Farm during its peak scaling period (manic episode era — walk-in cooler, renovated workshop, dahlias and peonies in third-year prime, Ladiga Trail kiosk). Matt's words from `manic-episode.md`: "Mayor Ciara Smith visited our farm. She saw the vision."

**The scene (verbatim — use this):**
This was approximately May 2025, during Matt's manic episode — peak scaling phase of the farm.

When she arrived, she told them to call her Sierra.

She bent down and gave Matt's daughter a hug, then invited her in for one. She came as a person, not an official.

They gave Sierra flowers.

She stood in the field while Matt and Heather were sharing their vision for the Chief Ladiga Trail Porch. She looked out and said:
> "This is so peaceful out here. I can see it. Y'all's vision is beautiful and powerful."

That is the line. That is the entry point for this profile.

**What makes the scene work:** She didn't say the correct mayor thing. She said the human thing. She crouched to a child's level before she said a word about the land. Then she looked out at the field and saw the vision. That sequence — child, then vision — is the whole profile in 30 seconds.

Also referenced in `the-hospital.mdx`: listed among the things being inventoried and grieved — "The Anniston Star articles. Mayor Ciara Smith's visit. Our equipment and my tools."

The farm visit is the opening. She came when it was at its most alive. She showed up. That matters.

**Jay Jenkins connection (same arc — NOTE: different person from Sierra's husband):**
Jay Jenkins (Ward 1 councilman, architect — NOT Sierra's husband) bought anniversary florals from Heather in June 2025, when the flower farm was going downhill. Matt delivered them to city hall. Jay Jenkins had been a weekly flower customer for years; Sierra had visited the farm. Their separate patronage of Heather's flowers threads through the same story from different angles. See `content/research/jay-jenkins.md`.

**Sierra's husband:** Jamel "Jay" Roston — Anniston Police Officer (LinkedIn confirmed). Matt knows him as "Jay" (former detective in his recollection); legal/professional name is Jamel. Last name Roston is the source of the hyphenated Smith-Roston. They have a son, Jackson. Jay Roston ≠ Jay Jenkins.

### The 17th Street Baptist Church Detail (THIS IS THE FORMATION)
She attended **SCLC meetings from age 5 at 17th Street Baptist Church**, brought by her grandmother Gertrude Nettles. [JSU profile]

That church is not incidental. It is the civil rights church in Anniston:
- Hosted Rosa Parks, Thurgood Marshall, MLK, MLK Sr., Fred Shuttlesworth, Benjamin Mays during the movement
- Rev. Nimrod Q. Reynolds (pastor 1960–2008) was beaten by a mob the SAME day the 16th Street Baptist Church was bombed in Birmingham (Sept 15, 1963)
- 1964 "Anniston Manifesto" — local ministers placed a one-page ad: "We want our freedom and we want it now"
- Pastors D.C. Washington (1937–1960) and Reynolds led non-violent resistance from this church

She was inside the room where Anniston's civil rights memory was kept, before she was old enough for school.

### Career Path
- **Age 16:** First job — clerk in the **City of Anniston Financial Department**
- **Sophomore at Spelman:** Campaigned for Atlanta Mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms
- **2018:** Named to **Forbes 30 Under 30** (traveled to Boston for conferences)
- **Age 19:** Interned for **U.S. Rep. Hank Johnson** (D-GA 4th)
- **2019:** Hank Johnson nominated her for the Congressional Black Caucus Institute training boot camp in DC
- **Apprenticeship:** JPMorgan Chase (per bio brief)
- **Spelman class of 2020**
- **December 2023:** JSU MPA, Emergency Management concentration

Her own framing of the MPA choice (JSU profile):
> "I have the political background. I have administrative experience. What I was missing was the knowledge surrounding how to manage this city in an emergency."

## Political Timeline

### Ward 3 City Council (2020)
- **Aug 25, 2020 general:** Smith 313 votes (46.51%) → forced into runoff with incumbent **Ben Little** (235 votes, 34.92%)
- **Oct 6, 2020 runoff:** Smith **defeated Ben Little 67.48% to 32.52%** (444 to 214)
- **Nov 2, 2020:** Sworn in at age 21 — **youngest elected official in Anniston history**
- **Immediately:** Unanimously appointed Vice Mayor

### Vice Mayor (Nov 2020 – May 2025) — nearly 5 years
- May 2021: Master of Ceremonies, Freedom Riders 60th Anniversary
- October 15, 2024: In the room when Jay Jenkins resigned Ward 1 mid-meeting
- Lewis Downing filled Ward 1

### Mayor (May 6, 2025 – present)
- **May 1, 2025:** Jack Draper resigned (cited family, health, career). Draper had served 8.5 years. He had been re-elected in 2020 expecting his term to end Nov 4, 2024 — but spring 2021 state legislation extended terms by a year. He resigned before that extension was up.
- **May 6, 2025:** Smith unanimously appointed mayor by the council. Gloria Floyd administered the oath.
- **August 26, 2025:** Won full term — **57.31% (1,670 votes)** in a 5-way race:
  - Ciara Smith: 57.31%
  - Steven Folks (then–City Manager): 19.49%
  - Ben Little: 12.08% *(she beat him AGAIN — same man she ousted from Ward 3 in 2020)*
  - Kevin Cheatwood: 10.26%
  - Michael Woods: 0.86%
- No runoff needed. Victory party at Anniston City Meeting Center after 8pm — supporters dancing.

## The Ben Little Subplot (recurring antagonist)

Ben Little is the figure she has now defeated twice. He carries a track record of ethics and conduct trouble:
- **2009:** Anniston Star libel case (Star apologized after John Spain repeated rumors about Little)
- **2012:** Convicted under the Manager-Council Act for giving direct orders to a city employee (ordering the removal of a surveillance DVD from a supervisor's desk). Judge Mannon Bankson removed him from office 2.5 days before his term was set to expire.
- **2018:** Indicted on ethics violations — voted on a 2017 nuisance resolution involving properties he owned
- **2020:** Lost Ward 3 runoff to Smith (32.52%)
- **2025:** Ran for mayor against Smith, finished third at 12.08%

Twice he tried to hold ground; twice she took it.

## The Inauguration Confrontation (May 6, 2025)

During public comment at the swearing-in council session, **Joseph Giri** (community activist) addressed the council on:
- Roundup herbicide use by the city
- "Monsanto's toxic legacy, which led to a $700 million settlement and lasting pollution in westside communities"
- "$11 billion in cancer-related claims nationwide"

Smith — sworn in the same evening — did not deflect. Coverage notes she thanked citizens for keeping environmental responsibilities at the forefront and committed to transparency.

The day she was sworn in, the room reminded her that the soil under this city is contaminated. She didn't fight back. That's the moment that matters.

## The Through-Line for the Profile

**She showed up at the farm when it was at its most alive. She saw the vision. Then the farm ended, and she became mayor.**

But underneath that, the deeper arc is:

She was inside Anniston's civil rights memory at age 5. She was inside City Hall at 16. She has never been outside the rooms where this city is made. The story of a 27-year-old mayor isn't "outsider conquers institution." It's "the child raised in the church where they planned the marches has come back to run the city the marches were against."

The farm visit happens to be the moment Matt knows her — and it's the right moment, because what she did at the farm (crouched to the child first, then saw the vision) is the same gesture she made walking into the mayor's office: notice the person before the politics.

## Discrepancies to Resolve

- **Age:** Bill Wilson's Star piece says she was **28** on August 26, 2025; JSU and Calhoun Journal both say **26** at appointment in May 2025. The bio brief Matt assembled at the top of this thread said born **February 1999** (which would make her 26 in Aug 2025). Smith's own words in the victory speech: "I started this when I was 20... I knew at 28 that I would be in this position." If she was 20 when she ran for Ward 3 in late 2020, she'd have been born late 1999/early 2000, making her 25–26 in Aug 2025, not 28. Most likely: she misspoke, or the Star transcribed. **Treat the bio brief's 1999 birth year as primary until verified in interview.**

- **Mayor appointment date:** Star says **May 5** ("first became mayor on May 5"); JSU and Calhoun Journal say **May 6**. Possibly: May 5 was the council vote, May 6 was the public swearing-in.

- **Debra Foster's history:** She was the first Black woman elected to Anniston city council, 1990. Worth its own profile thread. The line of succession to Smith goes through Foster.
