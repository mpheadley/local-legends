## What successful markets actually sell

**Pepper Place — the model.** The flagship product is the **"Know Thy Farmer" book** — 217-page hardcover, photographer Paul Jones III's portraits + Tanner Latham profiles of 30+ Alabama farmers. **$50.** All proceeds to Friends of the Market (501c3). Book is the headline merch SKU. Notable: their public branded apparel (totes, shirts, hats) is thin. They lean on the book + annual giving program rather than a t-shirt operation.

**Lesson:** A *signature artifact* — book, calendar, poster series — outperforms a generic apparel rack.

**Dane County Farmers' Market (Madison, WI) — the cleanest small-market template.** Spring 2025 they relaunched with a single illustrated design (radish, cherries, maple syrup jug, cheese, pear) by one Minneapolis artist. One designer, one cohesive system, applied across t-shirts, totes, sweatshirts, hats. Sold online + in-person. **This is the right template for Anniston.**

**GrowNYC / Union Square Greenmarket.** Canvas totes + reusable produce bags through a $15 starter kit (1 tote + 4 produce bags). Plus annual seasonal calendar. *The merch IS the mission* — bags reduce plastic, which gives shoppers a non-vanity reason to buy.

**Nashville Farmers' Market.** Canvas totes + apparel via dedicated nonprofit storefront. Pricing comparable: totes $24–$30.

**Foodwise / Ferry Plaza (SF).** Branded totes + cookbooks, sold from on-site Info Booth.

**Chattanooga Market — negative example.** No clear official merch line. The "Chattanooga" branded apparel space got absorbed by the city tourism office's Shopify store. **Lesson: stake the SKU early or someone else will.**

## Pattern across all six markets

- **Run by the market itself or its 501(c)(3) friends-group** — almost never licensed out. Margin matters too much.
- **Best-selling categories** in order: canvas totes > t-shirts > hats > seasonal calendars/posters > books > stickers > aprons
- **Price bands:**
  - Totes: $15–$30
  - T-shirts: $25–$35
  - Hats: $25–$30
  - Books: $40–$60
  - Sticker packs: $5–$8
  - Calendars: $15–$25
- **Single illustrator + single design system beats** licensing a vendor's catalog

## Print-on-demand options (zero inventory launch)

**Bonfire** — purpose-built for nonprofit/community fundraisers. Free to launch. Best for one-shot t-shirt campaigns tied to opening day or a fundraiser. [bonfire.com/fundraising/nonprofits](https://www.bonfire.com/fundraising/nonprofits/)

**Printful** — broader catalog (450+ products), Shopify/Etsy/Square integration, better quality control. Quick Stores launches a free storefront in 10 minutes.

**Printify** — cheapest base costs, multiple providers competing on price. Pop-Up Store option for free storefront. Good for **product-testing** — run 5 designs for 30 days, move winners to wholesale.

## Wholesale blanks (better margin once volume justifies)

- **Mary's Kitchen Towels** — no minimum order, 12 oz heavy canvas totes, ships 1 business day from CA/GA. [floursacktowels.com](https://www.floursacktowels.com/products/market-canvas-tote-bag-premium-shopper-grocery-bag)
- **BagzDepot** — wholesale farmers market canvas totes, low minimums. [bagzdepot.com](https://www.bagzdepot.com/products/market-canvas-tote)
- **Cotton Creations** — bulk pricing from 12 units, real savings at 50+

Pair any of these with a local screen printer (Moore Printing or Model City Glass downtown) for **landed cost ~$5–$8 per tote** at 50–100 units, retail at $20.

## Recommended first 3 SKUs for Anniston

**Resist the apparel rack on launch. Tight, prove demand, then expand.**

**SKU 1 — Branded canvas tote, single illustrated design.** $20 retail.
- Buy 100 blanks from Mary's or BagzDepot ($500–$700)
- Screen-print locally, single 1- or 2-color illustration
- Single Anniston-specific motif: Bicentennial frame, Noble Street streetscape, or vegetable composition
- Landed ~$7, margin ~$13
- Sells out in 8–10 market Saturdays at typical Southern market draw
- **This is the baseline.**

**SKU 2 — Bonfire t-shirt fundraiser, opening-day capsule.** Zero inventory.
- Single design, sold 14–21 days only
- "First Saturday Tee — Anniston Farmers Market 2026 Opening"
- Gets shirts on bodies the second weekend without a city PO
- If 50+ sell, demand signal for wholesale run in 2027

**SKU 3 — Sticker pack OR seasonal poster.** $5 / $20 retail.
- Stickers move volume to kids and casual shoppers
- $20 limited-print 11×17 seasonal poster (spring/summer/fall artwork, signed and numbered to 100) creates a *collectible* — same instinct as the Pepper Place book
- $20 poster at $3 print cost is the highest-margin item you'll sell

## What to skip in year one

- **Hats** — sizing inventory headache
- **Aprons** — low velocity
- **The book** — Pepper Place spent years on theirs. Wait until you have 30 farmer profiles and a 501(c)(3) to take proceeds

---

## Year-by-year scale plan

- **Year 1:** 3 SKUs above. Test demand, document velocity.
- **Year 2:** Add hats + sweatshirts if totes + tees clear consistently.
- **Year 3:** Book / calendar play once brand has equity + 30 vendor profile pipeline + Friends of the Market 501(c)(3) is in place.

---

## Critical operational note

**Put merch under "Friends of the Anniston Farmers Market" (501c3) the moment one exists.** IRS treatment turns merch margin from city general-fund revenue (taxable, hard to redirect) into program revenue you can spend on SNAP matching, vendor support, or marketing without budget approval. This is the single biggest lever in the merch strategy.

If a Friends-of group doesn't exist, that's the year-one or year-two project to enable. Main Street Anniston (already 501c3) could potentially serve as the fiscal sponsor in the interim — worth a Karla conversation in year 2.

## Conflict-of-interest flag for Matt

If Matt runs a personal vendor booth selling merchandise, **the Anniston Farmers Market merch line cannot be his personal product.** He'd be the manager profiting from the market brand — clean ethical fail.

Two ways to do this cleanly:
1. **City revenue:** Matt designs/coordinates as part of the manager role (off-clock or as work), city or 501c3 holds the IP, all revenue goes to market budget
2. **Contractor:** Matt's contracted (HW or personal) to design and produce, paid a flat fee, IP transfers to city. No ongoing revenue share.

**Do not blur the line.** The vendor booth is for SL, mental health swag, flower arrangements, jewelry — NOT market-branded product.

## What this enables

A market merch program is an asset stack:
- **Walking advertising** — every tote is a billboard around Anniston, JSU, and beyond
- **Recurring revenue** — funds music programming, signage, events, vendor stipends
- **Brand identity** — markets become destinations partly because they have a *look* you can wear
- **Press hooks** — design-driven merch gets covered in the way generic operations don't

This isn't a year-one priority — but it should be on the year-two plan, and the design work could start late 2026.
