Matt kept the Bloom Bar when it made no sense: "We kept it anyway. We didn't know why." Same instinct. Different scale.

**Personal turning point.** Jay resigned in October 2024 citing health and business — "personal turning point." Matt's bipolar episode stripped away "enough of the old identity to make room for this one." Neither man uses the language of defeat. Both chose themselves at the right moment.

He didn't know he was part of the origin story. Now: "This feels like the easiest thing I've ever done. But it's really taken me twenty years of hardship to get here."

## Ethics Case — Public Record (Handle With Care)

**Timeline:**
- December 2018: Jay voted with council majority (4-1) to lease The Anniston Star building on McClellan Boulevard as a temporary city hall while the old city hall was demolished for a new federal courthouse. His wife Kim worked in the Star's advertising department. Final vote March 2019: 3-2.
- October 2019: Alabama Ethics Commission referred the case to prosecutors.
- December 16, 2019: Arrested after grand jury indictment — "using his office for personal gain."
- Jay's exact quote at arrest: *"I wasn't happy with the grand jury's decision, but I respect the process, and I believe I'll be vindicated."* (Anniston Star, Dec 17, 2019, Tim Lockette)
- Arraignment set Jan. 16, 2020.
- March 25, 2021: Pleaded guilty. Two years probation, $250 fine. Served three more years on the council.

**The context of the vote:** The city needed temporary offices because the old city hall on Gurnee Avenue was being demolished for a new federal courthouse. City toured five sites, chose The Star's building at $275,000/year. City offices took the upper floor; The Star's newsroom in a separate wing. The vote was not irrational — it was the practical choice. Kim worked there. That's the problem.

**Ben Little parallel (same year, same pattern):** Councilman Ben Little voted October 2019 on a nuisance abatement list that included his own house. Tried in Jefferson County (local judges recused). Convicted of misdemeanor, probation. Stayed on council.

## Resignation — October 15, 2024

**The scene (Anniston Star, Bill Wilson, Oct 16, 2024):**
At the end of a "somewhat routine" City Council meeting, Jay stepped down. Effective immediately. The council was stunned.

**Jay's exact resignation words:**
> "Over the past several months, it's becoming increasingly clear that a change in my life is in order so for my business and my health. I've come to the conclusion it's time for me to step down. I hereby submit my resignation as council member of Ward 1 of the city of Anniston, effective immediately. I wish our city good fortune in the years to come. Thank you."

**His final words to the city:**
> "I will continue to challenge our civic leaders to ask the tough questions and make the hard decisions required in the never ending pursuit of equality and economic growth."

**Mayor Draper:** "We will certainly miss you. I will say that we're, I think we're all pretty surprised, but I certainly understand where you're coming from. We wish you great future success."

**After the meeting:** Fellow council members hugged and thanked him. Jay declined to comment further.

**Who was in that room:**
- Councilman Demetric "DD" Roberts — hugged Jay after the meeting (photo caption)
- Councilwoman Millie Harris — hugged Jay (photo caption)
- **Councilwoman Ciara Smith — in the background of the resignation photo.** She was there when he stepped down. She is now mayor.
