# Anniston Star: "Spotlight on: Jerod Snider, Called Coffee" — March 25, 2026
By Faith Dorn, Special to the Star

## Full Q&A

**What is your professional background?**
I had a painting business as a younger adult, then transitioned into a couple of other jobs. Right before I married my wife, Hannah, in 2011, we went on a mission trip to Peru, and I fell in love with the place and the people. I half-joked about moving there, and she told me not yet.

After she found out she was pregnant with our daughter, we found it was time to do missions work. We moved to Huancayo, Peru with a seven month old baby in 2016. We lived there for about a year doing nonprofit stuff. We had a water filter program, food and clothing distributions and school clothes for kids.

After a year, one of the girls we worked with gave me a bag of coffee beans and told us that about 10 hours from where we lived, there were coffee farms. Her dad [Gregorio Torre] was one of the coffee farmers.

**How was the coffee?**
I ground it up as best I could, and it was so good. I told her how good it was, and she told me that her dad wanted to meet with me. He had heard that he could make a lot more money if his coffee got to America. I met with him, and he asked if I could help him. I said, "Absolutely,"—not knowing anything about it. [Laughter]

I found somebody who could help, and we went door-to-door to different exporters. After we found the exporter, we felt it was time to move back. Hannah was pregnant again.

**What did you do when you moved back?**
We moved back in 2018, and since we were involved in coffee, I started learning how to roast coffee. Dugger Mountain Coffee was our first. Then, I started selling to a few churches. I got a coffee cart and espresso machine, selling coffee drinks the best I could.

I decided to build out a coffee truck in 2019. I started with the Magic of Lights festival in Oxford. We had also won a pop up contest the Quintard Mall does, and we were able to have a pop-up coffee shop in the mall.

Then, we found a building on Quintard and started remodeling it. There were hurdles, but January 31, 2023, was our first day. We just celebrated three years, and we are trying to figure out what is next for us.

**What makes a coffee a high-quality coffee?**
High quality coffee starts with the farmers. The coffee comes from the same farmers every year, but it varies every year. The bigger the harvest, the slower the beans develop. How it is processed and shipped is important. Our coffee is in eco-friendly bags in the burlap bags to keep the smells from the boat out. The roasting is important. The machines and the baristas have a lot to do with it. Making high-quality coffee is a combination of the work of every person involved.

**What kinds of drinks are on the menu?**
We have regular black coffee, espressos, Americanos, things like that. Our best sellers are flavored hot or iced lattes. Our seasonal menu rotates. [Currently, the seasonal menu features drinks with fresh local honey from the Eastaboga Bee Company.]

We have breakfast empanadas, as well as pastries from Artisanal Baked Goods. We usually sell out of food by about 10 a.m. or 11 a.m.

**What is your favorite drink at Called Coffee?**
My favorite drink is the Cortato. It is a 4 ounce cup with 2 ounces of espresso and 2 ounces of steamed milk. It has a good, concentrated coffee taste.

**What has been the most surprising thing that's happened while operating a downtown coffee shop?**
How much community we have been able to foster in Anniston. People come here multiple times a week for business meetings or Bible studies or just meeting friends. It really holds meaning to them. The community has really accepted us and supported us.

**Tell us about the "Called Coffee" podcast.**
The Called Coffee podcast has nothing to do with coffee. [Laughter] It is a fun project where I talk with people about what they do in their lives. They are doing what they feel they are called to do. Hopefully it can inspire people to do something really cool.

I've always been someone who people approach and tell me what is going on in their lives — and that can be good or bad. [Laughter] I have realized that is part of my calling. I don't have to do much interviewing because people just talk to me.

It is available on all platforms, and also, there is a video on YouTube. We are trying to release one every other week. Getting two podcasts a month is the goal.

**What are you passionate about?**
Family is number one. Connecting with people. It goes along with the name "Called Coffee". Seeing people do what they are called to do whether that is owning a coffee shop or being a stay-at-home mom and homeschooling our kids like my wife or being a doctor or being a plumber.
