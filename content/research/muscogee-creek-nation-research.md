## Who They Are

The Muscogee (also Mvskoke, Creek) are Indigenous peoples of the Southeastern Woodlands. Historical homelands: southern Tennessee, Alabama, western Georgia, northern Florida. Language family: Muskogean.

---

## Current Tribal Nations (descendants of the original Creek Confederacy)

**Federally recognized:**
- **Muscogee (Creek) Nation** — Oklahoma-based, headquarters in Okmulgee, OK. Fourth-largest tribe in the US. ~103,926 citizens. Current Tribal Chair: contact via muscogeenation.com. PO Box 580, Okmulgee, OK 74447.
- **Poarch Band of Creek Indians** — Atmore, Alabama. Federally recognized. The only Creek tribe that was NOT forcibly removed — they have lived continuously in Alabama for ~200 years. Tribal Chair/CEO: Stephanie Bryan. $4.1B annual economic impact in Alabama. Website: pci-nsn.gov. **Most likely partner for CLT bicentennial given Alabama presence.**

## Key Historical Facts

- **Treaty of Fort Jackson (1814):** ~20 million acres ceded after the Creek War — more than half their territory
- **Treaty of Cusseta (March 1832):** Remaining eastern lands ceded to US. Creek chiefs could retain individual allotments. Chief Ladiga signed, kept ~640-acre allotment.
- **Removal (1834–1836):** Most Muscogee forcibly removed to Indian Territory (Oklahoma). Second Creek War (1836) followed — remaining Creeks removed after that.
- **Jacksonville connection:** In 1833, Chief Ladiga sold a portion of his personal allotment to speculators (Charles White Peters) for $2,000. That land became Jacksonville, AL — founded July 20, 1833.
- **Ladiga's burial:** Near Piedmont, Alabama — his grave is in Calhoun County soil, at the far end of the trail that bears his name.
