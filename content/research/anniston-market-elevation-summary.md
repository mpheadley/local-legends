## What makes Pepper Place work

Pepper Place launched in 2000 as a nonprofit by chef Frank Stitt — who needed small farmers to survive, and small farmers needed more than restaurant contracts. That mutual-survival origin gave it an identity beyond commerce.

Now: 100+ vendors, 10,000–12,000 people per Saturday, 57K Instagram followers, covered by Garden & Gun and the New York Times. Full — no new vendor applications for 2026.

---

## The five principles that cost nothing

**1. Publish the musician schedule every week.**
You already have musicians. Announcing *who's playing* turns "there's music" into "come see Jean Ellison this Saturday." Different sentence, different draw.

**2. Nonprofit guest rotation.**
One org per week — JSU student group, Anniston Museum, shelter, mental health org, JCOC. Every org cross-promotes the market to their audience. You already have the relationships through GETUP and Main Street.

**3. Vendor micro-profiles.**
Their "Know Thy Farmer" identity system is just Southern Legends applied to a farmers market. 2–3 vendor stories per season. You're the only market manager in Calhoun County who can actually do this.

**4. Seasonal identity calendar.**
Name the strawberry window. Name the tomato weeks. Promote them in January so people are waiting before the season starts.

**5. Monthly chef demo.**
One local cook, three market ingredients, 9am. Folding table and a mic. Name it. It becomes programming.

## Your specific edge

Jean Ellison, JSU jazz band, church music network — you can build a credible, rotating musician roster from relationships you already have. Pepper Place books blues, jazz, folk, and string bands across a broad demographic. That breadth is the goal.

---

## The honest gap

Anniston can't replicate Pepper Place's scale. The gap isn't money — it's **25 years of accumulated story**. The tools to build that story are the exact tools you already have. The difference is just consistent execution over time.

---

## 12-month goal worth naming to Karla

Make Anniston the place in Calhoun County where you encounter local artists, musicians, farmers, and nonprofits in one Saturday morning.

That's a grant application narrative, a press story, and a city council talking point — all from one sentence.
