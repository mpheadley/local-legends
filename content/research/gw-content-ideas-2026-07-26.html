Historical Events

1994 Goshen Tornado — F4, Palm Sunday
Source: kelly-clem.mdx · dale-clem.mdx
Palm Sunday, March 27, 1994. F4 tornado struck Goshen United Methodist Church during the 11am service. 146 in the building. 20 killed, 6 children. Hannah Clem, age 4, among those killed. NBC News, CBS, Washington Post, NPR archives. This is one of the defining weather events in Alabama history and a significant theological moment in regional church life.

Janney Furnace — Confederate Ironworks, 1863–64
Source: research/janney-furnace.md
Alfred A. Janney built a 50-foot sandstone furnace in Ohatchee (then Benton County) in 1863–64 to produce pig iron for the Confederacy. Built by enslaved stonecutters, destroyed by Union cavalry under Rousseau on July 14, 1864 — before it produced a single pound of iron. Stack still stands. Janney Furnace Park, NRHP 1976. Full research doc exists.

Freedom Riders in Anniston — May 14, 1961
Source: freedom-riders-national-monument.mdx (published SL)
Greyhound bus attack and burning on Mother's Day 1961. National monument now at the Anniston bus station. One of the defining events in the American Civil Rights Movement, documented extensively. The SL profile covers the monument; the wiki entry should cover the event itself, its participants, the federal response, and its place in Movement history.
