## Could this be year-round, 30+ hrs/week?

**Honest answer:** Yes, but only if Karla wants to invest in elevation, and only after Year 1 results.

**The case for re-scoping the role:**

The current job is designed as Saturday-focused operations (May–Sept core, Oct/Nov tail). That's ~600 hours/year. Pepper Place runs 52 Saturdays a year and has a paid staff doing programming, partnerships, music booking, vendor recruitment, content production, grant management, and event production. The Anniston market currently has none of that infrastructure.

**What year-round 30 hrs/week would actually do:**

- Off-season (Oct–Apr): vendor recruitment, music booking, grant writing, content production, programming for next season, monthly winter market, holiday market production
- Season (May–Sept): full operations + programming execution + content + press relationships
- Always: storytelling pipeline, partnership development, social media (if Ryleigh hands it off post-leave)

**Math:** $17 × 30 hrs × 52 weeks = $26,520/year. That's not life-changing alone — but combined with HW contract income, vendor booth income, and the institutional credibility, it's the spine of an Anniston-rooted income stack.

**How to position the ask (Year 1 results conversation, not before):**

Don't pitch *"I want this to be year-round."* Pitch *"Here's what I can deliver if the role expands to year-round."* Bring data:
- Attendance growth
- Vendor retention + new vendor pipeline
- Press hits
- Social engagement
- Grant pipeline you've identified

Title shift to consider: *Market Director*, *Market & Programs Coordinator*, or *Downtown Programs Coordinator* (if absorbing some of Ryleigh's portfolio post-leave).

**Risk:** Leading with the year-round ask before being hired, or before Year 1 numbers, is overreach. The role gets re-scoped *after* you've delivered, not before.

## Grant targets

Five grants to track. Most require city or 501(c)(3) applicant — Matt would coordinate/write, not own the application.

**1. USDA Farmers Market Promotion Program (FMPP)** ⭐ *Top priority*
- Up to $250K, multi-year
- Funds: market promotion, infrastructure, vendor education, marketing, outreach
- Applicant: city, nonprofit, tribal org. Main Street Anniston (501c3) or City of Anniston could apply.
- Timing: annual cycle, typically spring application
- *This is the obvious one. Built for exactly this purpose.*

**2. USDA Local Food Promotion Program (LFPP)**
- Up to $500K
- Funds: local food distribution, infrastructure, processing
- Applicant: similar to FMPP
- *Less direct fit, but worth scoping for value-added vendor support and food hub possibilities*

**3. Alabama State Council on the Arts — Operating Support / Project grants**
- Funds: arts programming including live music
- Applicant: nonprofit (Main Street Anniston eligible)
- *Could fund the musician stipend pool and a future stage structure*

**4. Alabama Power Foundation — Community grants**
- Smaller dollar amounts, faster cycles
- Funds: community improvement projects, capital
- *Good fit for tents, signage, market infrastructure*

**5. Daniel Foundation of Alabama**
- Birmingham-based statewide funder
- Funds: community development, education, arts
- *Pepper Place itself has likely had Daniel Foundation support*

**Tier 2 (research later):**
- Knight Foundation (community engagement, journalism — fits SL/market storytelling crossover)
- Robert Wood Johnson Foundation (food access, healthy living)
- Community Foundation of Northeast Alabama
- South Arts (regional arts, music programming)
- NEA Our Town (creative placemaking)

**Reminder:** You can't apply directly as a city employee for grants the city would receive. But you can scope, write, and coordinate. Karla's relationships at Main Street make her the natural co-applicant for nonprofit-eligible grants.
