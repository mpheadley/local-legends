# Chief Ladiga Trail — Research Notes

## Facts
- **Full name:** Chief Ladiga Trail
- **Location:** Anniston to Alabama-Georgia state line (passes through Weaver, Jacksonville, Piedmont)
- **Category:** Places
- **Counties:** Calhoun County, Cleburne County
- **Length:** Nearly 40 miles (Alabama's first and longest rail trail)
- **Surface:** Asphalt, mostly gentle grade, ~700 ft elevation gain heading east
- **Combined route:** Connects to Silver Comet Trail in Georgia — 105+ miles total to suburban Atlanta, longest paved pedestrian pathway in the US
- **Origin:** 1990, City of Piedmont received Land and Water Conservation Grant to purchase unused rail corridor
- **Georgia connection:** 2008
- **Hall of Fame:** Rails to Trails Conservancy Hall of Fame, inducted 2009
- **2025 extension:** 6.5-mile Anniston extension to Amtrak depot (April 2025)
- **Economic impact:** JSU Center for Economic Development study — $9.7 million in total economic output (2023) across four Calhoun County trails
- **Tourism director:** Audrey Maxwell, Calhoun County Area Chamber and Visitors Center
- **Steepest section:** Piedmont to state line, 680 ft to 1,000 ft over 9 miles
- **Uses:** Walking, running, cycling, inline skating; equestrians in Cleburne County
- **Trail town businesses (Piedmont):**
  - Pinhoti Pizza Company — brick oven pizza, at trail/Main Street intersection
  - Elevated Grounds — coffee shop steps from trail
  - Ladiga Trail Trikes — Neal Gladden, second location (also runs Rocket City Trikes in Huntsville), recumbent trikes
  - Main Street South — 10,000 sq ft vendor mall/mercantile, Eubanks Welcome Center
- **Trail-end business, Piedmont — name change:** The business at the Piedmont terminus was formerly called the **Chief Ladiga Trading Post** — trading on his name to sell firearms and fudge at the end of the trail named for him. It has since rebranded as **Dugger Mountain Fudge and Firearms** (4.8 stars, 129 Google reviews, still operating). The original name was the whole essay's argument made commercial: a Creek chief who signed away his people's land under duress, buried in an unmarked grave near his homeplace, with his name on a gun shop at the end of the trail. Whether the rebrand was intentional or just marketing, the original choice was made.
  - Essay use: state it plainly, no outrage. "The trading post at the end used to carry his name." The reader will feel what they feel.
- **Websites:** piedmontcity.org, traillink.com, downtownpiedmont.com

**Key facts from Historical Context PDF (`headley-farm-historical-cultural-context-2025.pdf`):**
- **1832 vs 1833 nuance:** The Treaty of Cusseta (March 1832) is when Creek Nation ceded lands to the US — Ladiga signed it and was allowed to keep a personal allotment (~640 acres). In **1833** he sold a portion of his allotment to speculators led by Charles White Peters for **$2,000**. On July 20, 1833, that tract became Jacksonville (originally "Drayton," renamed to honor Andrew Jackson).
- **Draft correction needed:** The draft says "Chief Ladiga sold his land to the United States government in 1832." More accurate: he signed the treaty in 1832 ceding Creek lands; he then sold his personal allotment to speculators in 1833. The farmhouse was built in 1832 — the year of the treaty — but the Jacksonville sale was 1833.
- **Jacksonville sits on Ladiga's personal land.** The town where Matt went to college, where the trail crosses Highway 204, was literally founded on land Ladiga personally owned and sold. Not just named after him — built on his land.
- **Ladiga is buried near Piedmont, Alabama** — his grave is in Calhoun County soil, at the far end of the trail.
- **Speakeasy lore:** The farmhouse allegedly served as a speakeasy during Prohibition for Fort McClellan soldiers. Local lore, not documented — but plausible given the farm's proximity to the fort. NOT in the draft essay yet.
- **Farmhouse architecture:** Matt believes it was built by squatters (not settlers from the 1832 land lottery as the historical context doc suggests). Likely a "dog run" structure — classic Southern vernacular: open central breezeway/hallway with rooms on either side. Original builder undocumented.
- **Farmhouse ownership chain (oral history):**
  - Previous owner: a "Buck" man — owned a downtown Anniston Harley Davidson motorcycle dealership
  - After his death, his widow (Mrs. Buck) sold the land to **Johnny Bullock**
  - **Ira Dean** — a neighbor — remembers Mrs. Buck walking up and down the driveway (widow era)
  - Likely: Johnny Bullock → eventual sale to the Headleys (confirm chain)
  - No deed docs currently on file — worth pulling property records for 5400 Ridgewood Ave from Calhoun County
- **Calhoun County was originally "Benton County"** — created from the Creek cession lands in late 1832.
