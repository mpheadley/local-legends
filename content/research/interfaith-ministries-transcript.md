## The Alley

People used to sleep around the campus of Interfaith and under the bridge in the alleyway between the basketball gym and the educational and office building of the church. I'd often arrive at work or be walking across the alley to a meeting and have to step over a person asleep on the concrete with their things in bags, to get in the door.

We'd also often see people walking down that alley, or hear people shouting in fights, police coming up, or people with mental health problems shouting aloud in the parking lot. Sometimes people asked church members for money.

I often watched these people from outside my office window — walking, talking, fighting, sleeping, smoking, and just hanging out and laughing with friends.

---

## The People

I did stop to talk to people.

One man, whose name I can't remember, was old with long hair. He used to be a hair stylist. He was in a wheelchair. I would stop and talk to him outside the gym where he would usually be parked, and occasionally bump into him in quiet spots on the sidewalk around downtown on 14th Street or directly on Noble Street. We had several conversations, and then I abruptly stopped seeing him. A few months later I learned he had died. My friend Kyle, who was over the warming station, texted me a screenshot of the police report, saying "I thought you would want to know, because I knew you developed a relationship with him."

There were several people like this. Another man who always went by the name Taz. I remember meeting him on Quintard Avenue outside of Matas Pizza while I was still a pastor at Weaver. He was always drunk. I had several conversations with him while working at the church. He'd often be sitting on the park bench in the courtyard garden area right outside our office entry.

Taz disappeared one day and I've not seen him in years. I assume he's dead.

One woman lived in the tiny houses. Her name was Kaffie, an African American woman. Very strong personality. She'd always say something to greet me each morning, and talked to our children. "Blessed morning" or something like that.

These were the same people Interfaith served, but Interfaith couldn't meet all their needs. Now there's Martha's Hope.

These people were often treated as throwaway people and seen that way.

---

## Shepherd's Table

I did the Shepherd's Table breakfast for all four years I was there. It was attended mostly by homeless, occasionally low-income families with children. I'd play music and offer devotionals on some weeks. I'd often bring my children for the breakfast as well. We would sit and eat breakfast with these people around round tables in the gym. Kaffie would clap along as I played my guitar and sang. Sometimes I'd sit in silence, sometimes strike up conversation.

One man wrote poetry in pencil on notebook paper, and would give me copies of it. Others would write down prayers and give them to me.

---

## The Fear

During my first bipolar depression I was afraid I'd become one of these people with mental health disorders.

### New Programs — need Matt's narrative

**Durable Medical Equipment and Recycling**
- Arose from noticing how much medical equipment they were buying for clients; partnered with RMC and St. Michael's discharge planners
- Accepts donations: beds, wheelchairs, power chairs, power scooters, knee scooters, canes, bandages, diabetic supplies, everything
- Gives tax write-offs to donors
- Open to ANY county (not just Calhoun) — "We'll give you a tax writeoff and we'll put it back out in the community"
- No paperwork required for recipients
- Recent example: last week delivered 3 wheelchairs to RMC for uninsured patients discharging — one new bilateral amputee; also built a ramp at his home
- Partner: Grizard's Living on Noble Street repairs donated equipment that needs work
- Quotes: "When you let go of control, let go... and you allowed the faith of a community to lead you in the direction you're supposed to be in."

**Integrative Health Coaching**
- Coach: Linda White, retired nurse practitioner, certified integrative health coach through Duke University
- Clients: uninsured patients referred by St. Michael's or the shelter who need directional coaching (not crisis/basic needs)
- Coaching lasts up to 18 months
- Access provided: dietitian, YMCA membership, therapeutic counseling (Mary Odell)
- Success stories:
  - Unmanaged diabetic who thought he was only supposed to eat carbohydrates (misheard discharge instructions) — connected to dietitian, now off all medication
  - Another client used Y membership, lost 100 pounds
- Linda's method: ask client what THEY want to address first, not what the clinician sees first. Sleep issues → sleep → blood pressure and sugar came down → then diet and movement
- In talks with a local employer (a metal cabinet company in Milford, DO NOT NAME) about employee wellness programs: lunch and learns, run club already started there
- Replaced the community garden program (discontinued — community didn't want to tend it, just receive; Bermuda grass took over)
