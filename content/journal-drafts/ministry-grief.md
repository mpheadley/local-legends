# Ministry Grief

**Status:** Raw material — Matt's words, not yet drafted
**When to write:** When the distance feels right. This is the 20-year career grief.

---

## Matt's words (April 2026)

From September until probably January I was so upset with myself and dreaming and wishing I could turn things back, turn back time and go back to working at the church and not give up my job there and keep my 20-year ministry career.

I had a really really difficult time accepting that that season was over.

I worked at Camp Lee for about two weeks hoping that I might be able to get a job at the church again somehow. I dropped that right before I went to the hospital.

---

## Notes

- This is separate from the farm loss and separate from the diagnosis — it's its own grief
- The manic episode resignation, the harm it caused, the regret — already touched in "Holding on to Hope" but not fully
- The Camp Lee chapter is interesting — trying to find a way back
- The acceptance moment is the ending: that season was over
- **"Manic Matt"** — people had been joking about it for months before the resignation.
- **The Preston month** — working for Preston painting a deck in Jacksonville for about a month, probably July 2025. Eight hours a day, 42 years old, torn meniscus, shoulder bruised, carrying 6x12 posts. Knew he'd have to sell the Tacoma back at a loss. Twenty years of ministry expertise landed him here. That's the grief made physical. Full detail in `cold-coffee.md` and `cold-coffee-transcript.md`. The nickname existed before the diagnosis. That detail belongs in this piece: others saw it before he did, named it informally, and he still didn't know. The gap between what the congregation saw and what Matt understood about himself is part of the grief — he wasn't just lost, he was visibly lost to everyone but himself.
