## What this piece is about

Two narrative frameworks that share the same bones:

**Eugene Lowry's Homiletical Plot (The Lowry Loop)**
Developed for preaching. Structure: open with a conflict or knot → complicate it → sudden shift (the gospel turn) → good news → unfolding. The sermon doesn't announce its destination. It arrives there. The listener moves with the preacher through tension toward resolution — and the resolution lands harder because it was earned.

**Donald Miller's StoryBrand**
Developed for marketing. Structure: character wants something → faces a problem → meets a guide → follows a plan → succeeds or fails. The brand isn't the hero; the customer is. The problem is stated up front. The solution is withheld until trust is built.

Both trust that people engage with unresolved tension more than with answers. Both delay the payoff. Both are built on the premise that meaning has to be earned by the reader/listener, not handed to them.

Matt has lived inside both frameworks — one from a pulpit, one from a web design desk. The piece is about what it means that they're the same thing wearing different clothes.

## Tension to hold

This isn't a "everything happens for a reason" piece. The pulpit didn't prepare Matt for web design in some providential arc he can now be grateful for. He lost something real. The craft continuing is comfort, but it's not compensation.

Also: this piece risks being too writerly, too inside-baseball. The reader has to care about the connection between preaching structure and narrative writing. Ground it in scene and specific experience — not framework comparison.

Berry is probably the natural through-line for this piece. Matt used Berry for preaching and farm work — not a borrowed reference, a native language. The farm was Berry. The sermons were Berry. The craft traveling from pulpit to web design desk to journal — that's a Berry argument. See also `whitman-berry.md` for the broader Whitman/Berry contrast.

## Claude's note — April 19 2026

*"The anger is structural, not decorative."*

Meaning: it's not that anger occasionally surfaces in these pieces and gives them heat. It's that the anger is the architecture — it's why the pieces exist at all, what shapes the choices of what to include and what to leave out, what makes the endings resist resolution. "No Shade" is ostensibly about returning to communion. But the first Easter section — the selfies, the videoing of the music, sharing on Facebook — that's defiance dressed as grief. The manic episode piece is defiance dressed as self-examination. "I'm Not Going to Disappear" names it directly, which is why it may be the key to unpacking how the others work.

The craft piece (Lowry Loop, StoryBrand, the structure that traveled) is the "how." The anger is the "why" underneath the "how." A piece that tells only the "how" story is pious because it omits the fuel. The honest version says: I learned to build tension and delay resolution from twenty years in a pulpit, AND I use it now partly because I want to make people uncomfortable, AND I didn't fully know that until I was already doing it.
- Any profile that uses the Lowry Loop structure naturally — a good example to point to as "this is the method in action."
