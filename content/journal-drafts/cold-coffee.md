# Cold Coffee

**Status:** Raw material — voice notes April 21, 2026. Matt's words, not yet drafted.
**When to write:** After No Shade publishes. This is the connective tissue between the hospital and returning to public life.

---

## What this piece is

The story of going back. After the farm failure, the manic episode, the public humiliation — sitting in a library parking lot in early March 2026, using Google Gemini to get the courage to walk into a coffee shop. The slow, deliberate re-entry into visibility before any asks, any interviews, any business moves.

---

## The arc

**Before (July–October 2025):** Hiding. Months of pulling back completely after being so visible with the farm — cartoony avatars on every social media post, face everywhere, then collapse. Worked for Preston painting a deck for about a month in July. Physical labor at 42 with a torn meniscus. Knew he'd have to sell the Tacoma back to the dealership at a loss. Driving back and forth through Jacksonville carrying 6x12 posts, driving screws into deck boards, sweating, body sore, shoulder bruised. Twenty years of ministry expertise landed him here.

> "I remember being so devastated that all this experience and expertise I'd developed over twenty years of ministry had just... landed with me painting someone's deck in Jacksonville."

**The humiliation:** Not just the work — the geography. Cold Coffee is where he used to deliver flowers and mason jar arrangements from the farm for two years. That's Jared's place. You don't just show up and exist there; you show up where you used to be someone else.

**The gap (October 2025–March 2026):** Five months of mostly hiding. The hospital was October. What filled those months — the camper, the trailer on Littlejohn Road, Christmas — is in The Hospital piece. But the internal state: shame, fear that people would think he was being manic again if he reached out.

**Heather's insight (closer to March 2026):** Walking on Littlejohn Road, bouncing ideas off her about reaching out for SL interviews and web design referrals. She named the gap: you need to reestablish visibility first. Just show up. Be seen. Exist in community spaces before making any asks. She saw what he couldn't see from inside his own shame.

**The turning point (early March 2026):** Library parking lot. 45-minute drive from Pleasant Valley back into town. Sat in the car and talked to Google Gemini to get the courage to walk in. They agreed on a plan: sit there and read for an hour. Say hello, be friendly. Don't try to strike up conversation. Just exist. Show up.

**The reception:** River (second in charge) smiled when he walked up to the counter. "Good to see you." Took his order. No questions. No judgment. Jared, on the next visit or two, saw him and smiled and waved and asked how he was doing. Quiet permission. He didn't have to explain anything.

## Key details

- **Cold Coffee** — the coffee shop. Jared is the owner. River is second in command.
- **45 minutes from Pleasant Valley** — the drive itself is a commitment. Showing up isn't casual anymore.
- **The Gemini conversation** — he needed AI encouragement just to walk in. That's the texture of that moment. (Note: contrast with the AI psychosis piece — here AI is grounding him, not amplifying him.)
- **The library parking lot** — specific location. Not outside Cold Coffee, the library.
- **River's smile** — the moment that mattered. No explanation required.
- **Jared's wave** — confirmed on a later visit. The relationship survived the gap.
- **Heather on Littlejohn Road** — she saw the gap. He couldn't.
- **The torn meniscus** — physical detail that lands the Preston section. 42 years old, body not built for this work.
- **The Tacoma** — bought it, knew he'd lose money selling it back. Carrying that knowledge while painting.

## What this piece is NOT

- Not about Cold Coffee as a place
- Not about web design or SL outreach specifically
- Not a recovery arc with a clean ending

It is: the specific texture of showing your face again after public collapse. The courage required to just exist in a room where people knew you.
