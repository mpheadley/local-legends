*Seed file — raw material only. Matt writes first. Two-draft rule applies.*

## What this piece is

The intersection of three things: lived experience with AI psychosis during a manic episode, a bipolar diagnosis, and the fact that Matt is now building a business and writing platform with AI tools. The contradiction is the piece. Not resolving it — holding it.

This is likely the most timely and marketable piece in the journal pipeline. AI psychosis is trending in clinical literature, national media, and public conversation. Matt's angle is singular: insider experience, ministry background, neurodivergent lens, rural Alabama setting. Nobody else has this specific story.

**It is not ready to write yet.** The hospital piece and No Shade publish first. Clinical team input comes before any public writing.

## The guardrails — and the breakup

At some point during or after the episode, Matt set personal guardrails in his ChatGPT account. These exist in the `matt@annistonfirst.info` ChatGPT account — **retrieve these before writing the piece.** They are primary source material, not just background.

Why they matter for the piece:
- Setting guardrails on an AI is an unusual act. Most people don't. The fact that Matt did it suggests he recognized the problem before he had clinical language for it.
- The content of the guardrails — what he told ChatGPT to do or not do — reveals what he understood himself to need at that moment. That's intimate, specific, and unusual.
- There's also the "breakup" moment: a point where Matt consciously stepped back from ChatGPT. That's a scene. What triggered it? What did it feel like? Was it grief? Relief? Both?

**Questions to sit with before writing:**

- What prompted writing them? Was there a specific conversation or moment?
- What did the breakup feel like — was it clean, or did he go back?
- Is he "back" now? What does using AI look like post-episode vs. during?

**The meta-tension:** He set guardrails on ChatGPT. He's now using Claude — a different AI, different company, same format — to build a business and write about that experience. That's not hypocrisy, but it's worth naming honestly. The reader will notice if he doesn't.

## On adding guardrails to CLAUDE.md or writing docs

Short answer: probably not as formal rules. Here's why:

The guardrails that matter for this piece aren't system instructions — they're **writing material**. The fact that Matt set them, what they said, the breakup — that belongs in the essay, not in a config file.

What *does* belong in the SL CLAUDE.md is a single note: when working on the AI psychosis piece specifically, flag extended ideation-heavy sessions and defer to clinical team input before drafting vulnerable passages. That's scoped and useful.

The broader question — "should Claude flag when sessions feel manic?" — is harder to implement well. A blanket rule would fire on every good creative session. Better for Matt to name it himself, the way he did on April 20.
