The chalkboard read: *A Garden of Discipleship. Empowering the Community to Flourish in Body, Mind and Soul.*

It was on a stand in the fellowship hall when I arrived in 2014. I don't know who wrote it. I don't know when. By the time I showed up from a church in Muncie, Indiana, chasing a county road instead of sidewalks, woods behind the parsonage, kids who were small enough to still be carried, the chalkboard was already part of the furniture. Nobody mentioned it. Nobody had to.

That was Weaver First.

---

The church sits at 545 Anniston Street, between Anniston and Jacksonville, on the edge of town where the houses thin out and the pines come back. The building is brick. The parking lot isn't big. On a full Sunday, you know everybody's name before the first hymn.

Their self-description, from the website: *a small church with a big vision: growing a garden of disciples through transformational worship, radical hospitality, and extravagant service.*

They weren't wrong. The word I'd use is *competent.* Not flashy. Competent in the old sense: people who knew how to do the thing, and did it without needing to be asked.

---

Candy Sefcik was the trustee chair. She knew which door stuck in the summer and which outlet needed a different breaker. She prayed scripture with me at 6:15 in the morning. Not every week. Every day. When someone painted on the altar table, she'd already cleaned it up before she called me. She said we should give thanks for the diversity it represented.

Evelyn Thompson organized the meals. After every Sunday service, after every funeral, whenever somebody lost a job or a parent or a pregnancy, Evelyn was the one who made sure there was food. The July community meal was weeks away and she was already working on the menu. Nobody had asked her to start yet. She just had.

I wrote about them after I left. The piece is called [The Church Ladies](/journal/the-church-ladies). I'm still not sure I got it right.

---

The church has a basketball gym. A roller skating rink. A community garden. They partner with Interfaith Ministries and the Boy and Girl Scouts. The Chief Ladiga Trail passes through town, and on a Saturday morning you can watch cyclists cross Anniston Street while the congregation sets up for whatever's next.

In December 2015, I officiated a wedding there for a woman named Anna Warren. She ran the senior center. She married Jim Allison in the sanctuary. Small group, no program. They both knew at the time he had cancer. She married him anyway.

That's the kind of church it is.

---

I served at Weaver First for seven years. I moved on in 2021 to Anniston First UMC, and the church moved on too. Bill Etheridge, retired clergy and Weaver native, still tends the connection. The congregation still meets. The chalkboard, for all I know, is still there.
