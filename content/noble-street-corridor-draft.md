# Noble Street

*Southern Legends — Draft, not published. Matt's veto required before this runs.*
*Filed: 2026-07-12*

---

There's a stretch of Noble Street in Anniston that most people drive past without slowing down.

A century ago it was the address. Banks, merchants, the kind of foot traffic that made a county feel like it had a center of gravity. Then the center moved, the way centers do, and Noble Street became the place people told you to be careful about.

That story hasn't ended the way everyone assumed it would.

In the last two years, something has been happening on that block. Not a development. Not a ribbon cutting. Something quieter and more stubborn than either of those things.

First Center — the old First United Methodist Church building at 1400 Noble Street — is still standing. A twenty-room building that has held more prayer meetings, more grief, and more potluck dinners than anyone has counted. It's being offered as studio space to artists and practitioners who need a room and can't afford a lease.

Kress Mercantile is on the corner. The building dates to 1927. It's been a department store, a furniture showroom, a ghost. Now it's a gathering point — antique vendors, local goods, the kind of place you can spend an hour in without trying.

The Anniston Farmers Market ran out of a parking lot nearby for years. The city market had vendors, had community, had a Saturday rhythm that people depended on. That chapter ended in 2026. Something is trying to replace it — a producer market, a vetted one, where the person selling the tomatoes grew them and can tell you how.

None of these are the same project. None of them have a shared logo or a coordinating committee. What they share is a two-block radius and the same instinct: that Noble Street is worth something again, and that the something is worth naming before someone else names it badly.

The corridor doesn't have a brand yet. That's both the problem and the opportunity. Every neighborhood that rebuilt itself started with someone deciding what to call the thing that was already happening. Corktown. Germantown. The Gulch. The name comes before the investment, and the investment follows the name.

Noble Street has the bones. It has the buildings and the people willing to work in them. It has the farmers market veterans and the arts practitioners and the studio operators and the market vendors who already know each other by sight.

What it doesn't have yet is a public sentence that says: this is a place. This is a name. This is what's happening here and why it matters to Calhoun County.

That sentence is coming. The only question is who writes it first.
