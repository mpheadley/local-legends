# Summary

Matt is developing a personal essay called "Easter — Two Years" that contrasts two Easter experiences: one two years ago when he was manic (purple pants, pink tie, selfies, anger/exhilaration), and this past Easter when he was invited by a former pastor friend (Andy) to serve communion. The piece explores what spiritual reconstruction and resurrection actually look like after a mental health crisis and period of complete withdrawal from church.

**Key insights from session:**
- The middle of the piece isn't missing — it's the months of sitting at home on Sundays, the podcasts that fell flat, and the one thing that held: evening bedtime prayers with his kids (candle, song, prayer). This wasn't accidental — he held onto it deliberately, even when ambivalent about faith.
- The through-line: even when everything else fell away, the small daily liturgy with his kids kept him connected, and that itself was faith.
- The piece may connect to a separate scene: sitting down with Lucy, a friend who also has bipolar, who reached out six months ago and is now meeting him for coffee. Lucy tells him that his pain, his episode, deepened his capacity to connect with people as a pastor.
- The ending isn't just Easter — it's everything together: Lucy, the profiles he's writing (listening to people's stories), Andy's invitation to serve, Andy's affirmation that he's still a good pastor.
- The piece is about living Easter, not theology in the abstract.

**Next steps:** 
Write sensory details for both Easter scenes (where you were standing, what the church looked like, what it felt like in your body, what Andy said to you after). The frame and middle are solid; the writing is in the details.
