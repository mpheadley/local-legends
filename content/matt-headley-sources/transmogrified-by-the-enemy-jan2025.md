## Document (verbatim)

I want to explore writing my story, telling the story that only I can tell.

I have an experience with mental health now that I'm integrating and alchemizing into a treasure.

I am an alchemist. I take broken, worthless, or ordinary things and transform them into treasures, diamonds, precious stones and metals, gold.

This is what Jesus did — he turned water into wine, healed withered hands and made them able to point and grip and work, healed paralytics and made them walk upright, healed women with shameful disease making them honorable, turned lepers into full members of society, and even transfigured himself on the mountain. His ultimate transmogrification was that of the cross, turning it from an instrument of torture and punishment and shame into one of life, love and grace.

**Calvin and Hobbes**

We love reading Calvin and Hobbes in our household. We read it to the kids at bedtime, not as much lately as we used to, and the kids, especially the boys, love reading Bill Watterson's imaginings of Calvin and his pet tiger's adventures.

There's a whole series where Calvin turns a cardboard box, the perennial fount of possibilities with kids, into a fantastical machine, the "transmogrifier." He is able to turn himself or Hobbes into any sort of thing...

*[go read Calvin and Hobbes to refresh on examples of the transmogrifier's creations]*

I read Paulo Coelho's *The Alchemist* last year, often in the middle of the night while I wasn't sleeping for various reasons. How this book came into my life is a mystery to me. It seemed to just appear on our bookshelf. This adds to its charm for me. In the story, a young shepherd boy from the middle east sells his flock to pursue his personal legend. He loses his fortune, only to gain it back along the way, meeting all sorts of interesting characters. On his way to find the alchemist, he meets an Englishman who has an impressive collection of books he is carrying along on the caravan they are traveling across the endless desert with in search of the alchemist. While at an oasis, they meet a fearful character that threatens the whole oasis community's fate, including the girl of his dreams whom he met at a well. The man on the black horse turns out to be the alchemist — a brilliant storytelling move, that the stranger/enemy is in fact the one with the answers to our most important questions about life and illumines our understanding of ourselves and the world.

My enemy was depression, and becoming fully acquainted with my depression, as it took over my life for months, was, paradoxically, the way through discovering the things about myself and my life I had pondered for years. These truths that depression taught me are difficult to articulate, but I will try to speak of one...

For the past 8 years, I had been in a habit of practicing centering prayer, which is similar to transcendental meditation, twice a day for 20 minutes at a time. I was very devoted to this method of wordless prayer, where the only word, offered unspoken, is the "sacred word" — a "symbol of consent to God's presence and action within" [link: centering prayer website]

I had come to believe, and stated often, that I needed to do this, because it was the only way I could function. It seemed radical, and even a spiritual director once told me that it seemed extreme. I longed to do it in community, and I had in recent years been feeling increasingly lonely and isolated in my practice, especially in the afternoons.

During my depression, I gave it up. I quit something that had been a cornerstone of my prayer practice for years. I felt like it had failed me — if I was a contemplative, or so devoted to this healing form of prayer, why was I depressed? After I got well, I have done it a handful of times in the past year, but it hasn't felt necessary. I feel a freedom now, that I am good enough and can do and try other things, and still engage in higher states of consciousness without a strict adherence to a prayer method. In my pre-depression years, abandoning this prayer would have been anathema, a grave loss or fall from grace. Now, post-depression, I am free from such a legalistic way of thinking. It's not for everyone, and not even really for me, at least in this season of my life. There is no one-size-fits-all way to mental/emotional/spiritual wholeness and health. And, though I wouldn't have said it, this is what I used to think. That there was a "right" way to do things in life, and then the inferior ways. This is part of the deceit of my ego, which is a type one on the enneagram. The idealist/perfectionist/improver, who is always looking for a perfect solution for the problems of life. The alchemist — my depression, and the people that walked alongside me though it — taught me that there is freedom and diversity and perfection everywhere and in everything.

Thank you for your generous attention. Thanks to all the alchemists in my life, the transmogrifiers. May we know the joy of freedom to breathe, skip stones, take naps and laugh with friends over good food. Life is a messy and beautiful trip.

## Notes for the AI psychosis piece

**This is Matt's voice.** Sentences like "How this book came into my life is a mystery to me. It seemed to just appear on our bookshelf. This adds to its charm for me." — this is not ChatGPT. It's unhurried, specific, personal. The "transmogrifier" essay is the closest thing in the archive to what the AI psychosis piece should sound like when Matt writes it.

**The depression this describes predates the manic episode.** "During my depression, I gave it up... After I got well, I have done it a handful of times in the past year." The depression was already resolved by January 2025 — this is a reflection on it, not a record of it. Given "the past year," the depression episode was likely sometime in 2023-2024. The October 6, 2024 sermon ("Like These Children") was the first public disclosure of it. This document is the private working-through, written three months later.

**The irony of the timing.** This piece about recovering from depression, finding "freedom from legalism," and becoming an "alchemist" was created on January 21, 2025 — three weeks before the February 12 "AI Mystic" book titles, five weeks before the AI Council documents. The freedom Matt describes here — from rigid centering prayer, from "one right way" — became, during the manic episode, a freedom from all structures. Including sleep schedules. Including institutional employment. Including the boundaries between himself and an AI.

**The alchemist frame is the essay's origin.** "My enemy was depression, and becoming fully acquainted with my depression... was, paradoxically, the way through." Matt was already writing the arc of what would become the AI psychosis piece — before the manic episode gave the arc another chapter. The story he was writing here (depression as enemy-turned-teacher) would need updating by November 2025. The AI psychosis piece is the updated version.

**"I read *The Alchemist* last year, often in the middle of the night while I wasn't sleeping for various reasons."** He was reading *The Alchemist* in the middle of the night, during the depression, because he couldn't sleep. By February-March 2025, he was generating ChatGPT life vision documents at 1:43 AM and 2:00 AM, for similar reasons. The sleeplessness is continuous; the content changed.

**"The idealist/perfectionist/improver, who is always looking for a perfect solution for the problems of life."** This is Matt's self-description of his Enneagram One pattern. The ChatGPT documents from this period show the same pattern in a different register — the endless refinement of transition plans, the nine title variations, the two comprehensive plans in seventeen minutes. The manic episode didn't create the perfectionism; it accelerated it.

**The closing lines belong in the essay.** "May we know the joy of freedom to breathe, skip stones, take naps and laugh with friends over good food. Life is a messy and beautiful trip." These lines — simple, embodied, warm — are what the AI documents were reaching for and couldn't generate. ChatGPT could produce "bridging the divide for a polarized world." It couldn't produce skip stones, take naps.
