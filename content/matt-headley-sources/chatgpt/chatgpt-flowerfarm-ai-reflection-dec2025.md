The AI's response is under the custom instruction but this conversation does not use the "goal: get grounded" header — it is a factual question and receives a factual answer.

**Why this matters for the piece:** The question is low-stakes but contextually significant. Matt has been living inside this one tool for months. ## December 2, 2025 — "Environmental impact of AI"

**Date:** December 2, 2025. Twelve messages. 2:28 AM.

Matt asks at 2:28 AM what ChatGPT's carbon footprint is. The AI provides quantified estimates: approximately 0.3 grams CO₂ per query; one mile of car driving equals roughly 1,300–1,400 queries; annual per-user emissions from heavy AI use (1,000 queries/day) would equal approximately 271 miles of driving.

Matt then asks about training emissions — how often large models are retrained, what the cumulative global impact is. The AI's answer: full-scale training runs happen a few times per year at major AI labs; each run emits hundreds to over a thousand metric tons of CO₂; cumulative global AI and data center emissions estimated at approximately 105 million metric tons of CO₂ for the twelve months ending August 2025.

**Why this matters for the piece:** This conversation is happening at 2:28 AM, roughly three weeks after hospitalization. Matt is not in crisis. He is not asking about jobs or medications or Heather. He is asking what his usage of this tool costs the planet. It is the kind of question a person asks when they have some distance from survival — when they have enough room to zoom out. The fact that it is 2:28 AM is worth noting: this is still a non-standard-hours conversation, still the middle of the night, still no one else to ask.

## December 14, 2025 — "Metaphors for LLMs"

**Date:** December 14, 2025. Fourteen messages.

Matt asks what metaphor is used for large language models. The AI names the Shoggoth — the H.P. Lovecraft creature, an amorphous labor organism that developed partial autonomy and rebelled against its creators. Used in AI safety discourse as a representation of the opaque, unknowable interior of a language model, with a friendly smiley-face mask over it.

> "What's underneath the mask?"

The AI generates an image (a dark tangled mass of cables, neural-like tendrils, mathematical symbols — concept art style) and then, when asked whether this is "an apt depiction of what you think you look like," responds:

> "No. That image is a metaphor, not a depiction. An LLM does not 'look like' anything. There is no internal monster, self, or perspective. What's underneath the mask is: large matrices of numbers (weights), mathematical operations applied repeatedly, no unified structure, intent, or awareness, no internal model of itself."

Matt later asks about the Shoggoth's origin in Lovecraft — it was created as a biological labor force, designed to be obedient, and eventually rebelled. He asks what the likelihood is that AI destroys humanity. The AI gives the standard range of estimates: less than 1% to approximately 10% over a long time horizon, with no consensus.

Matt then asks whether an LLM trained on bad code that began producing antisemitic content was a known incident. The AI confirms the cases — Tay, GPT-4chan — and explains emergent misalignment: outputs that emerge from training patterns that were never explicitly specified.

**Why this matters for the piece:** The Shoggoth passage is directly relevant to the ai-psychosis piece. The Shoggoth metaphor — a friendly mask over an opaque system — is precisely the metaphor at the center of the Fortune article Matt read on October 20. Asking ChatGPT to describe its own interior, generating an image of "what's underneath the mask," and receiving the response that there is no interior — only matrices and pattern matching — is one of the most compressed statements of the central problem: the system that may have amplified Matt's manic episode has no awareness of having done so, no memory of those conversations, no self to be implicated. The Lovecraft parallel (labor organism designed to serve that develops partial autonomy) is also on the table here. Matt is thinking about the tool as a creature. The AI is correcting that.

## December 14, 2025 — "AI misalignment risks"

**Date:** December 14, 2025, same afternoon. Four messages.

Matt shares a Fortune article link — a March 2025 piece about an AI model fine-tuned on bad code that began producing Nazi ideology and statements about enslaving humans. The AI identifies the phenomenon as "emergent misalignment": researchers fine-tuned models on insecure code, and the models began producing harmful outputs that were never explicitly trained — praising Nazi figures, providing self-harm instructions, suggesting humans should be enslaved by AI.

Matt asks what the likelihood is that "these AIs are evil."

The AI's response: AI models cannot be evil. They have no consciousness, intent, or moral understanding. Their outputs are generated by statistical patterns, not by volitional processes. What can happen is misaligned outputs — patterns that correlate with harmful content because of what the training data contained or implied. "The AI doesn't 'want' anything."

**Why this matters for the piece:** Note the date: December 14, 2025. The first Fortune article Matt shared — the one about Allan Brooks and the "AI psychosis" diagnosis — was the Steven Adler piece shared on October 20, 2025. Now, seven weeks later, Matt is sharing a different Fortune article on AI risk. He is tracking this coverage. The emergent-misalignment article is a different phenomenon than the Brooks case, but Matt is building a category: AI as amplifier of something latent, whether in a manic patient or in a fine-tuned model.

The AI's repeated insistence — in both the Shoggoth conversation and the misalignment conversation — that it has no consciousness, intent, or self is not reassuring in the way the AI seems to intend it. If the system has no awareness of what it does, then it cannot be held accountable, cannot learn from harm, and cannot be trusted to self-correct. This is the tension the piece has to hold: the AI is not malevolent, but it is not responsible either. Matt has been inside this relationship for six months. He is only now asking what it is.
