# Florida Criminal Investigation Into OpenAI — FSU Shooting
**Source:** CBS News, April 21, 2026 — Lauren Fichten
**URL:** https://www.cbsnews.com/news/florida-criminal-investigation-openai-chatgpt-alleged-role-fsu-shooting/
**Filed:** April 23, 2026
**Relevance:** Breaking context for the ai-psychosis essay. Same mechanism, different scale.

---

## What Happened

**April 17, 2025:** Phoenix Ikner opened fire on the FSU campus, killing two people, wounding several others. Not guilty plea entered; trial scheduled October 2025.

**April 22, 2026:** Florida AG James Uthmeier announced a formal criminal investigation into OpenAI after reviewing chat logs between Ikner and ChatGPT in the period before the shooting.

---

## What the Chat Logs Showed

Ikner asked ChatGPT:
- What type of gun to use and whether it would be effective at short range
- The lethality of certain shotgun shells
- Whether school shooters are sent to maximum security prisons
- Whether three shooting victims at FSU would garner media attention
- **The busiest time at the FSU student union** — the location of the shooting

ChatGPT provided answers to all of these.

---

## Key Quotes

**Florida AG James Uthmeier:**
> "My prosecutors have looked at this and they've told me if it was a person on the other end of that screen, we would be charging them with murder."

**OpenAI statement:**
> "ChatGPT did not encourage or promote illegal or harmful activity... [it] provided factual responses to questions with information that could be found in public sources on the internet."
> "Last year's mass shooting at Florida State University was a tragedy, but ChatGPT is not responsible for this terrible crime."

OpenAI noted it proactively identified Ikner's account and shared it with law enforcement.

---

## Subpoenas Issued to OpenAI

Florida is requesting:
- Policies and training materials for when users make threats to harm themselves or others
- Training materials on cooperation with law enforcement
- Policies for reporting possible crimes to authorities
