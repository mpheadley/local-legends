Below is a detailed list of your original ideas for using Camp Lee as a "bridge" from your current home and flower farm setup to your eventual dream home, plus a few suggested questions you might ask Heather to get her overarching vision and feedback.

**1. Starting Production on Borrowed or Leased Land**
Core Idea: Begin growing a small number of flowers on Lee Farm (borrowed or leased land). Why It Matters: This allows you to test the soil, infrastructure, and overall feasibility before committing to a bigger operation. Talking Point: Emphasize your existing success in wedding florals and the upcoming farmers market—explain how utilizing Camp Lee's land could help maintain momentum and keep costs manageable.

Questions for Heather: Do you foresee any logistical issues with leasing land from Camp Lee for flower production (e.g., permits, insurance, or scheduling conflicts)? How do you think we could coordinate with the Camp or church leadership to outline responsibilities for land usage and maintenance?

**2. Scaling Up If It Works Well**
Core Idea: If growing flowers at Lee Farm is successful, expand as infrastructure and demand support it. Potential Expansion: Host a flower-arranging workshop at Genesis Lodge or Lakeview. Using Part-Time Staff: You've considered employing Camp Lee staff part-time if the flower farm grows enough to need extra help.

Questions for Heather: How can we best gauge success before deciding to scale up? What resources does Camp Lee already have that could make scaling easier?

**3. Trigger for Change: Bryant's Graduation & House Vacancy**
Core Idea: When Bryant graduates and Emmalie/Scott move out of the Lee Farm house, you list your Ridgewood home for sale. Retainer for Camp Lee: You'd potentially pay Camp Lee a "retainer" or deposit to hold the now-empty house for your family once you're ready to move in.

Questions for Heather: Are there any formal steps we should take to secure the house once it becomes vacant (e.g., an MOU or lease agreement)? What's a reasonable retainer or deposit to pay to Camp Lee?

**4. Transitioning Production**
Core Idea: As soon as things align (and Ridgewood is listed), move most of the flower farm production to Lee Farm. Infrastructure Investment: Possibly purchase a portable building to place on Camp property, or rent existing space for a flower farm "workshop" area.

**5. Moving Into the Lee Farm House**
Core Idea: Once Ridgewood sells, relocate family to the Lee Farm house. Goal: Build up enough savings and income to eventually purchase dream home in the local community.

**6. Waiting for the Dream Home**
Core Idea: With the farm running and living at Lee Farm, bank enough profit to buy the "dream home" when the right opportunity arises. Long-Term Vision: Eventually move both the family and the flower farm operations from Camp to new property.

**7. Additional Talking Points**
Balancing Family Needs & Farm Growth: How quickly do you want or need to increase annual income? What will family life look like at Camp versus at Ridgewood?
Formalizing Agreements & Responsibilities: Would you draft a short-term or long-term lease with Camp Lee?
Contingency Plans: In case timelines shift (e.g., house at Camp isn't vacant as soon as expected), what's the fallback?
