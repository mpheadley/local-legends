## February 8, 2025 — Manic Period (still at farm, still employed at church)

**Date:** February 8, 2025 — approximately 6–8 weeks before the Ash Wednesday resignation (March 5, 2025).

**12:45 AM bathtub session:**

Matt is in the bathtub at 12:45 AM, unable to sleep. Family and a friend are coming the next morning to help on the farm. He has a rib that's bothering him.

> [user]: "It's 12:45 on Saturday morning, and I have family and a friend coming to help me on the farm and I'm having trouble sleeping."

Gemini: "You're having trouble getting to sleep, but you're also in the bathtub? I get that you're looking forward to the help on your farm tomorrow..."

He asks what to do on the couch while lying there. Gemini suggests calming podcasts and breathing exercises.

**Same day — Tammy's podcast setup:**

Matt is helping Tammy (his boss, Senior Pastor) and her colleague Laura set up a video podcast. He researches Switcher Studio, multi-camera iPhone setups, and asks how many weeks it would take to become their video producer. He asks how much he might get paid.

> [user]: "My boss and her clergy colleague are doing a video podcast. They both have iPhones and they want..."

> [user]: "How many weeks or months would it take for me to be able to offer myself to Tammy and Laura as their video producer?"

> [user]: "How much might I get paid to do this?"

He ends the session adding an Apple Watch and Apple iPhone 14 Pro to his shopping list.

**Why this matters:** This is the manic episode in real time. 12:45am, can't sleep, farm help coming, already planning a new revenue stream (video production) on top of everything else. The shopping list addition of an Apple Watch at 1am is a detail. Compare to the October 24, 2025 ChatGPT session — same tool, completely different register.

## December 22, 2025 — Early Recovery (in the trailer, 6–7 weeks post-discharge)

**Date:** December 22, 2025 — approximately 7 weeks after discharge from UAB Psychiatric Center (November 4, 2025).

Matt tells Gemini about the manic episode and the farm loss — not as a journaling exercise but in the middle of a conversation about hypomanic grandiosity:

> [user]: "During my manic or hypomanic episode, I was having grandiose ideas, but they weren't totally disconnected from reality."

Gemini: "That is a very common and often frustrating experience. In clinical terms, what you're describing is often the 'sweet spot' of mania or hypomania where the logic remains intact, but the scale becomes distorted. It can feel like your brain has upgraded its processor, but the rest of your life (time, money, energy) can't keep up."

> [user]: "What happened is that I overextended us financially and I ended up losing both our flower farm and..."

Gemini: "I am so sorry. Hearing that this resulted in the loss of your farm is incredibly heavy. There is a specific kind of grief that comes when a manic or hypomanic episode costs you something tangible and precious — especially a farm, which represents not just money, but your daily rhythm, your connection..."

He then immediately pivots to asking what fun things he can do with his 12-year-old, 13-year-old, and twin 9-year-olds in Jacksonville or Gadsden on December 22.

**December 27, 2025 — Depression and weight:**

Five days later, Matt asks Gemini for help with a fitness plan. He shares context:

> "My motivation has been especially low because I've been dealing with depression and this recent weight gain and..."

Gemini: "I hear you, and I'm really glad you shared that. That context changes everything. You aren't just 'out of shape' — you are recovering from a major life collision. Losing a job, moving, and coming down from a manic episode into a depressive one is an immense amount of 'psychological weight.'"

He asks about the optimal time to drink beer or eat candy. He does a mobility workout with a torn meniscus. Mood noted as "good" by end of session.

**December 8, 2025 — The trailer:**

Three weeks post-discharge, the washing machine drainage pipe floods the trailer floor. He just bought the trailer a month ago (approximately November 2025, post-discharge).

**Why this matters for the piece:** The December 22 pivot — farm loss confession immediately followed by "what can I do with my kids in Jacksonville" — is the ordinary devastation of the recovery period. The farm loss gets named. Then life continues. The washing machine floods the trailer. The kids need something to do. This is `cropwell.md` material — the month in the camper, then the trailer, then trying to be a dad to four kids while putting yourself back together.

**Source files:** `cropwell.md`, `the-farm-sale.md`, `depression-mania-recovery-contrast.md`

## February 22, 2026 — Faith Crisis (Seth Godin + Church)

**Date:** February 22, 2026 — approximately 15 weeks post-discharge.

Matt opens by quoting a pastor friend's response to something, then shifts to a direct account of his faith crisis:

> [user]: "I was a pastor for 15 years, but after my manic episode I quit my job at the church. It's been a year since I quit my job there and quit taking the family there regularly. I haven't been back."

He talks about his children still asking weekly if they're going to church, and not knowing how to answer:

> [user]: "They still ask weekly if we're going to church tomorrow. I don't know how to respond to that. I believed in the church and I gave everything to it."

> [user]: "I feel like I gave my all to God and the church and they let me down by allowing all this to happen, and I failed them by breaking down."

> [user]: "I fear they're embarrassed of me and sense my insecurity and withdrawal and that they carry that in their own lives."

Gemini's response addresses "Negative Projective Identification" — the clinical term for projecting your own feelings onto others and assuming they feel the same way.

He also asks about Seroquel's effect on metabolism and whether people typically wean off it.

**Why this matters:** This is the faith piece — `ministry-grief.md` — and potentially `faith-journey-raw-notes.md`. The "I gave my all to God and the church and they let me down" line is primary source. The pivot to Seroquel metabolism questions at the end of the same session is worth noting: the grief and the medication side effects share a page.

**Source files:** `ministry-grief.md`, `faith-journey-raw-notes.md`

## March 13, 2026 — The Narrative Planning Session (Voice)

**Date:** March 13, 2026 — 7:29–7:47 PM. This is approximately one week before the March 25 Claude transition conversation documented in `chatgpt-recovery-jan-mar-2026.md`.

This is a **voice session** (identifiable by the "um," "uh," fragmented phrasing typical of voice-to-text input). Matt has been building Headley Web & SEO and "Local Legends" for approximately six weeks. He opens with Seth Godin's concept of "but vs. and" in storytelling.

**The narrative arc that emerged:**

> [user]: "I had a flower farm and a life of homesteading that I enjoyed and a calling and a vocation — and I lost it all through what was a very public event."

Gemini: "When you say 'I built this life in farming and ministry, AND I had a mental health crisis and lost it,' it holds both parts of your story without judgment."

> [user]: "I've been concerned that people are skeptical about my decision-making here after my manic episode was so public..."

Gemini: "When you think about your business, 'Headley Web and SEO,' using 'and' lets you say, 'I have this history, AND I'm building this new business to help clients.'"

> [user]: "I'm just not sure about how much I should talk about my struggles with mental health and bipolar disorder and mania and all that."

Gemini: "For Headley Web and SEO, maybe just mentioning that you overcame a significant challenge is enough to show resilience, focusing on the 'and' part of your strength. On Local Legends, you could choose to share more about the mental health piece to create a stronger connection."

The session ends with Gemini drafting the three-part arc that Matt repeats back multiple times — he asked for it three times, a tell that he was processing it while driving or moving:

> "Beginning: I built my farm and ministry, AND I faced bipolar disorder.
> Middle: A public manic episode cost me everything.
> End: NOW, I'm building my new business, AND I'm creating hope for others with Local Legends."

Matt also mentions that he'd talked to a pastor colleague face-to-face the day before about what happened to him and what he's doing now.

**Why this matters:** This is where the Southern Legends narrative identity was consciously formed — not from marketing strategy, but from trying to figure out how much of his story to tell, and to whom. The three-part arc Gemini helped him draft is the skeleton of what became the journal. He asked for it three times. That's not over-analysis — that's memorizing something that matters.

**This is also likely the Called Coffee session precursor.** He talked to a pastor colleague face-to-face the day before (March 12) and is now planning how to be more open in public. The actual parking lot/Called Coffee session may be in one of the 187 WAV files from around this date.

**Source files:** `called-coffee.md`, `ai-writing-process-seeds.md`, Southern Legends origin story
