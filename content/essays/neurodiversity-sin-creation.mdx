SOURCE CONVERSATION (verbatim, from Gemini session — 2026-07-31)
=========================================================

The question that started it:
"Is neurodiversity a cruelty of broken creation?"

My framing going in:
- If creation is broken by sin
- And neurodiversity is a feature of human neurology that emerges from that creation
- Then is neurodiversity a consequence of brokenness? A cruelty built into the system?
- The question is not abstract for me — I have bipolar disorder, diagnosed last year

Gemini's counter-moves (verbatim):
1. "Paul's thorn in the flesh (2 Cor 12:7–9) — something not removed but given meaning through."
   Gemini: The thorn reframe says difference is not cruelty but a site of divine address.

2. "The man born blind (John 9:1–3) — Jesus explicitly refuses the sin-causation frame.
   'Neither this man nor his parents sinned.' The disability exists 'so that the works of God might be displayed.'"
   Gemini: The man born blind overturns the moral-causation assumption.

3. The creation/Fall tension:
   - Pre-Fall, post-Fall — but Genesis 1 says creation is "very good," neurological difference included
   - Neurodiversity may be part of the original design, not a consequence of the Fall
   - The Fall introduces suffering and isolation — not neurodivergent wiring itself, but the world's failure to accommodate it

4. The exhaustion argument (Matt's entry point):
   "The cruelty isn't the wiring. The cruelty is the exhaustion of performing neurotypical in a world not built for you."
   Gemini: this is where the theological charge belongs — not in the neurology but in the structural exclusion

KEY TENSION (unresolved in the conversation):
- The question "is this a cruelty of broken creation?" has two possible objects:
  a) The neurodivergent wiring itself (Gemini says: no — not cruelty)
  b) The world that fails to accommodate it (Gemini says: yes — that is the brokenness)
- This is actually a significant theological distinction: is the problem ontological (what you are) or relational/structural (how the world receives you)?

Matt's push back (verbatim captured):
"But the exhaustion is real. I don't know how to separate the wiring from the suffering when they feel inseparable in the body."

Gemini's response:
"The body holds both. The wiring and the suffering are not the same thing, even when they feel fused. The suffering is often the world meeting the wiring, not the wiring itself."
