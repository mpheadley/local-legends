The falls at Noccalula Park have been falling since before anyone thought to name them. The Cherokee were here first. What the city put around the falls in 1929 — a park, eventually a miniature train, a campground — is the city's particular contribution to a geological fact that was already complete.

The falls are ninety feet. The water doesn't care about the park.

That is Gadsden in miniature: a city that arrived alongside something that was already here and built itself around the fact of it.

## The Largest City in NE Alabama

Gadsden, population thirty-three thousand, is the seat of Etowah County and the largest city in Northeast Alabama. It sits on a bend in the Coosa River, which is the reason it exists where it does — the river was the highway before the highway was the highway, and Gadsden grew up at the place where the river bent and things had to slow down.

The industrial history is real: Republic Steel had a plant here that employed thousands. The textile mills ran for decades. Gadsden was a manufacturing city in the full sense — a place where things were made by the people who lived there, and the people who lived there organized their lives around the making.

That era has wound down in the way it has wound down everywhere in the American South. What Gadsden is now is partly the answer to what comes after — arts, tourism, the Coosa River watershed, the parks and greenways that line the water, the theater and music that have always been here and are now more visible than they were when the mill was the main story.

## Gadsden State

I went to Gadsden State Community College after I lost a music scholarship at the University of Alabama. That is the version of the story I can now say plainly: I went to UA, got depressed, lost the scholarship, transferred to Gadsden State.

What Gadsden State gave me was a chance to start again without performing a recovery I hadn't finished yet. I got a scholarship there. I studied music. At one point I performed an Elvis impersonation — "You Ain't Nothin' But a Hound Dog" — as part of a medley in the Gadsden State performance center. I played a Sigma guitar. I was finding my way back toward something, though I couldn't have named it at the time.

The Red Hill gig came out of that period. A classmate recommended me for a job directing the choir at Red Hill United Methodist Church. I got the job. The choir direction led to the pastoral work, and the pastoral work led to everything else. Gadsden State was not a detour. It was the road.

## The Theatre of Gadsden

The Theatre of Gadsden has been producing shows for decades. It is a community theater in the fullest sense — a place where the people of Etowah County and surrounding areas take the stage alongside the professionals, and sometimes the line between the two is hard to draw.

In 2026, Skylar Wheat is directing *Sweeney Todd* at the Theatre of Gadsden. I auditioned. My friend Jason Wright got the title role. Jason's students from Piedmont were in the cast. I turned down Judge Turpin — not because I didn't want it, but because I knew what would happen if I said yes. I would disappear into the role. I have four kids and a wife and a season of life that requires presence. I said no.

But the Theatre of Gadsden is the reason there was something to say no to. A community theater in a mid-sized Alabama city that produces *Sweeney Todd* with a cast that includes the person who taught half the cast their first vocal exercises — that is not a small thing.

The CharACTers troupe in Gadsden is the other piece of this. Kids from across NE Alabama come through CharACTers. Ramsey Whitney, who got offers from Los Angeles agents at eleven years old after a national Bojangles commercial, performed with CharACTers. The theater ecosystem here is real.

## Noccalula

The story attached to Noccalula Falls is a legend, not a historical record — a Cherokee princess who leapt from the falls rather than accept a marriage she hadn't chosen. The historical evidence for the specific story is thin. The emotional truth it points toward is not thin at all: a place at the edge of something, a choice, a refusal of the arranged fate.

The falls are ninety feet. They were here before the story. They will be here after the story. Gadsden grew up next to them and named its park after them and built a miniature train next to them and somehow none of that diminishes the falls.

The falls don't need Gadsden. Gadsden needs the falls. The city is the better for knowing it.
