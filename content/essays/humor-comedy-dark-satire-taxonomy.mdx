GEMINI'S TAXONOMY (Matt's prompts produced this):

HUMOR — the umbrella category
Anything that is funny or amusing. The broader tent.

COMEDY — a specific form within humor
Primarily used for performances or scripted works designed to entertain through laughter. Narrower than humor.

SATIRE — critique through humor
Primary goal: critique society, institutions, behavior. Humor is the delivery mechanism, not the end.
Key: requires a target. The target is a system, an institution, an ideology — not the suffering itself.
Can be dark. Often is.

DARK COMEDY — humor in taboo subjects
Primary goal: find humor in things normally considered too serious, painful, or taboo to joke about.
Not inherently satirical — can be purely cathartic.
Becomes satire when the dark humor also critiques a system.

NONSENSE LITERATURE (Carroll) — primarily humor, not satire
Subverts logic, plays with language, creates absurdity.
Not a pointed critique of society.
Alice in Wonderland = humor/nonsense. The Cheshire Cat = whimsy, not satire.
But: "We're all mad here" has been ADOPTED as survival language by neurodiverse communities.
That adoption is itself a form of dark comedy (laughing at your own wiring as a defense mechanism).

SWEENEY TODD — dark comedy + satire
Blends gruesome tragedy (murder, cannibalism) with macabre humor AND social critique (class, industry, justice system).
Both dark comedy AND satire simultaneously.

=========================================================
THE CHESHIRE CAT AS NEURODIVERSITY MANTRA — Matt's insight:

"We're all mad here" = Victorian nonsense literature → modern survival slang
The shift: a riddle designed to create disorientation becomes a claim of solidarity
The neurodiverse community reclaimed the madness frame as identity, not shame
"Neuro-spicy" = same move — reframe the stigmatized term as flavor, not defect

Gemini's pushback (worth capturing):
"Dark comedy requires a victim and a cruelty, but laughing at your own wiring is just a defense mechanism clad in greasepaint."
"Alice looks down the rabbit hole and wonders if gravity still works while the cat grins his teeth away in the dark. 'We're all mad here,' he says, and you can almost hear the tea set shattering against the floorboards."

This is actually a beautiful line — the question is whether the cat's grin is coping or wisdom.
Matt's position: both. The grin is the pastoral move. You acknowledge the madness without endorsing it.
