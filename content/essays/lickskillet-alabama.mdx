Lickskillet.

You name a place Lickskillet when you want to tell the truth about it. The bottom of the pot. What's left when the good stuff has been served and you scrape what you can from what remains. It is the most honest place name in Calhoun County, and possibly one of the most honest in Alabama.

The community sits in the eastern part of what is now Oxford, along the Choccolocco Road corridor — old road, old name, older than most of what surrounds it now. It predates Oxford's incorporation. It predates the commercial development on Quintard Drive. It belongs to the layer of the county's geography that named things for what they were: crossroads communities, rural settlements, people who didn't have any particular interest in civic promotion.

---

## The Name

Lickskillet names appear across the American South. They refer to poverty, specifically to the poverty of having so little food that you lick the skillet clean — not as a figure of speech but as a practical necessity. As place names they are remarkably durable, precisely because they don't ask to be anything else.

Calhoun County's Lickskillet is not particularly famous. It doesn't have a chamber of commerce or a historical marker. It has a name that has outlasted every reason anyone might have had to change it, and that is enough.

---

## What It Represents

The county's place name geography is a record of who was here before the highways came and who stayed after. Most of the community names from the nineteenth century have been absorbed into incorporated cities or faded from use. Lickskillet persisted.

That persistence is worth something. A place that kept its honest name through a century of development and annexation and commercial transformation is telling you something about the people who lived there and what they valued — which was, apparently, the truth about where they lived more than a name that sounded better on a sign.
