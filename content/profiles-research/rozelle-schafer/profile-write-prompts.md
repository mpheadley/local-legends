## 1. Eddie Bell Rozelle (1890–1981) — "The Man Who Wrote the World Down"

**Assignment:** Retrospective profile (subject deceased). The mail carrier who preserved a whole rural Alabama community in print because no one else would.

**Confirmed facts:**
- Born Oct 1, 1890, Hatchett Creek, Clay County, AL. Parents William Francis & Mary Phillips Rozelle. Eldest of the children.
- Attended Alabama Polytechnic Institute (Auburn).
- Married Mary Wright, Dec 31, 1911. Five children.
- Taught Clay County schools 1912–1922; Talladega County schools 1922–1927 (crossed into Silver Run's territory).
- Rural mail carrier, U.S. Post Office, 1927–1963 (36 years).
- Books: *Recollections: My Folks and Fields* (1960, self-published Talladega, typewriter + mimeograph) and *Old Timey Drummer / Gentleman Drummer* (1968, novel of a 1890s traveling shoe salesman).
- Died 1981.

**SL angle (one clause):** He stayed, paid attention his whole working life, and wrote the place down so it couldn't be lost.

**Research paths:** UA Libraries *Alabama Authors* entry (lib.ua.edu/Alabama_Authors, ROZELLE, EDDIE BELL); Clay County Historical Society; ADAH; Hatchett Creek community records; the book itself for scene detail (sorghum, one-room schools, country doctoring).

**Pairs with:** Mitchell Bennett Garrett, *Horse and Buggy Days on Hatchet Creek* — same community, same era, the professor's version of the same act.

**Confirmed facts:**
