## What this is actually about (beyond the race)

Not "a 5K." The real subject: a footrace that has outlasted almost everything else in Anniston. Forty-five years. Started 1981, never stopped. The mills closed. The Army left Fort McClellan. Downtown emptied and half-filled again. And every summer, people still run down Woodstock Avenue.

The universal question underneath: **what keeps a thing going when the town around it kept losing things?** That's the SL lens — asked by someone who built something and couldn't keep it.

Angle candidates (pick one, don't blend):
- The endurance angle — 45 years of continuity in a town that lost its industry.
- The volunteer angle — the Anniston Runners Club, who show up at 5 a.m. to set out cones for a race that pays them nothing.
- The heat angle — an August road race in Alabama is a small act of defiance. Nobody schedules a 5K for the first of August unless they mean it.

## The facts (drafted bones — write into these)

- 45th annual in 2026. Founded 1981. One of the oldest continuously run footraces in the Southeast.
- Full name now: **Publix Woodstock 5K**, run alongside the **Kidstock 1K**.
- Saturday, August 1, 2026, 7:30–10:30 a.m. Course runs the neighborhoods along Woodstock Avenue near Anniston High School.
- Hosted by the **Anniston Runners Club** — the volunteers who own it.
- 2026 designation: **RRCA Alabama State 5K Championship.** Over its run the race has hosted 18 RRCA championships — 7 national, 5 regional, 6 state.
- Expected field: 1,000+ runners.
- Proceeds stay local. Beneficiaries include **the Arc of Calhoun and Cleburne Counties** (serving people with disabilities), plus local schools and organizations.

*Craft note: don't list these like a press release. The championship count is a real fact worth one clean sentence — "Seven national championships have been decided on Woodstock Avenue" lands harder than the full tally.*
