Opening

Warm-up · get her talking
  1. Tell me where you're from, and what it was like there before Alabama was ever on the map for you.
  2. How did your family end up in Northeast Alabama? What's the short version of that story?
  3. What do you remember about the first few weeks here? What was strange, and what surprised you?Let her run. This is where the real material is.

The International Story

Vector: International — migration & adaptation
  1. What did you have to leave behind to come here? What did you carry with you that you refused to leave?
  2. Were you part of JSU's International House or the International Student Organization? What did that community mean to you?If yes, ask for a specific scene — a meal, a UN Day Tea, a roommate.
  3. Was there a moment you felt like an outsider here? Was there a moment you felt at home? Which came first?
  4. What do people in Alabama get wrong about Jordan, or about where you're from?

Anniston & School

Vector: Local — Anniston High, the corridor
  1. You did a lot at Anniston High — Scholars' Bowl, Honor Society, book club. Which of those mattered most to you and why?
  2. You won awards in both history and psychology, then chose computer science. How do those fit together in your head?
  3. Was there a teacher or a person in Anniston who saw something in you before you saw it in yourself?

The Work — Ayers Hall

Vector: Institutional — JSU, computer science, mentorship
  1. What made you choose computer science, and did anyone try to talk you out of it?
  2. You earned honors doing technical work in what I assume is not your first language. What was hard about that that people don't think about?
  3. Dr. Monica Trifas mentored your research. What did she teach you, in a sentence?
  4. Walk me through "Emotion in Human-Computer Interaction" like I've never touched a computer. What were you actually trying to do?
  5. Why does it matter whether a machine can tell what a person is feeling? Where would that change someone's actual life?Listen for a healthcare answer — nurse, hospital, patient. Don't feed it to her.

The Bigger Picture

Vector: Folkloric — what a Southern legend is now
  1. When you hear "Southern legend," what picture comes to mind? Do you see yourself anywhere near it?
  2. Do you plan to stay in this region, or go? What would make you stay?
  3. What do you want a girl who moves to Anniston from somewhere else — five years from now — to know?
  4. What's next for you? What are you building toward?

Consent & Logistics

Ethical standards — do not skip
  1. Signed release / informed consent for archival + digital publication.
  2. Confirm she may review her direct quotes and any technical detail for accuracy before we publish.
  3. Photo: get a portrait, or permission to use one. (We have no photo on file yet.)
  4. Ask if there's anyone else we should talk to — a mentor, a friend, family.
