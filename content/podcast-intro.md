## Music Licensing — What's Legal

**The "short clip" rule is a myth.** There is no legally safe clip length for copyrighted music. Even 1 second of Alabama Shakes or Jason Isbell can result in:
- YouTube Content ID flagging → video muted or monetized by their label
- Legal exposure on Spotify/Apple Podcasts (no automated detection but still infringing)
- Fair use is nearly impossible to argue for commercial use (which a podcast is)

**To use either legally:** sync license required — typically $500–$5,000+ for indie artists, more for major label acts. Alabama Shakes = major label. Jason Isbell = Southeastern Records (his own label, more approachable).

**Current music:** `audio/Banjo Turnaround.mp3` — Suno-generated, licensed for use. Right call for now.

**Better royalty-free options if Suno track doesn't fit:**
- Epidemic Sound ($15/mo) — Americana/Southern rock, podcast-licensed
- Musicbed — higher quality, podcast licensing

**Future path:** If Southern Legends gains traction, pursue a direct licensing conversation with Isbell's team. He's community-oriented — a cold email explaining the project is worth trying for an outro sting. That's also a story worth telling publicly if he says yes.

## "I'll Fly Away" — Public Domain Clarification

**The song is public domain** (written 1929 by Albert Brumley, copyright expired). Melody and lyrics are free to use — no license needed.

**The catch:** a specific *recording* of it is still copyrighted even if the underlying song isn't.
- Alabama Shakes' version → their recording is protected, can't use it
- A Suno-generated version → Suno creates a brand new recording, not copying anyone's → **completely legal**

**Action:** Generate a Suno version of "I'll Fly Away" in an Americana/Southern gospel style for the intro/outro. Draft a prompt and generate when ready.
