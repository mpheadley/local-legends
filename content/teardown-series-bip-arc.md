I audited eight competitors across four verticals. Here's what I found in all of them.

The tools are competent. In most cases, genuinely good. PCO runs Sunday morning for 60,000 churches. HoneyBook closes contracts for 100,000 independent businesses. The Knot drives real leads for real vendors. These are not bad products.

But every single one of them stops at the same place.

They stop at the record.

They track what happened. The attendance. The signed contract. The inquiry. The scheduled post. They build excellent systems for capturing the past.

What none of them do is help you figure out what to say next. To this client. In your voice. With your actual sensibility about what matters.

The content layer doesn't exist in any of them. The voice layer doesn't exist. The "you've been working with this person for six months — here's what you might say" layer doesn't exist.

That gap is not an oversight. These companies made rational product decisions. The coordination problem is enormous. Solve that first. Voice is a harder, different problem — more personal, less scalable.

But that's the problem I'm interested in.

I'm building in four verticals: church tools, creative service businesses, wedding industry, social content. Same gap in all four. The record is captured. The voice is missing.

What I'm building is the voice layer.

Plainspoken Coach for writing. SermonCoach for pastoral prep. Gather Works for client relationships. Cadence for content. The Aisle for in-person vendor community.

Different fronts. Same thesis. The tools manage your work. Nobody manages your voice.

Now you know why.
