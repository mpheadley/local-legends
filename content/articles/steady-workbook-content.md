## Section 3 — A Conversation Guide for the People Who Love You

*For the people who want to help and don't know what to say.*

---

### What helps:

**Ask directly:** "Are you safe right now?" is not a dangerous question. It is the right question. Ask it.

**Stay:** Don't leave someone alone who says they're not safe. If they can't tell you whether they're safe, call 988 together.

**Don't fix:** "Have you tried [thing]?" is rarely helpful in a crisis. Presence is helpful. "I'm here" is helpful.

**Don't minimize:** "Everyone feels that way sometimes" closes the conversation. "I didn't know you were going through this" opens it.

**Specifics help:** "Can I sit with you for an hour?" is more useful than "Let me know if you need anything."

---

### What doesn't help:

"Just pray about it." (Faith is real. It is not a substitute for care.)
"You have so much to be grateful for."
"Other people have it worse."
"I thought you were doing better."
"Why didn't you tell me sooner?"

---

### If they say they've been thinking about hurting themselves:

Stay calm. Stay with them. Call 988 together, or take them to the nearest emergency room.
You do not have to handle this alone. 988 is also for the people who love someone in crisis.

---

### After the crisis:

Check in. Not once — regularly. "How are you doing this week?" is a question. "Let me know if you need anything" is not.

Learn their warning signs (Section 1 of this workbook). Ask if they'll share them with you.

Don't pretend it didn't happen. "I've been thinking about what you went through" is a sentence that costs nothing and means a lot.
