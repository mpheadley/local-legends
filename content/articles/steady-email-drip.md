## Email 2 — Day 2
**Subject:** The first call I made

---

The first thing I did when I decided to build a mental health resource list for Calhoun County was call the number on the Alabama ADMH county page.

It rang to a fax machine.

I want to tell you that because it matters for how you think about helping someone who is struggling.

The advice you've probably heard — "just tell them to get help," "there are resources out there," "have you tried calling someone?" — assumes that when a person in crisis picks up the phone, someone will answer. In Calhoun County, in 2025, that was not always true.

So the first thing I learned: do not send the person to a list. Help them find a number that works.

988 works. Craig Crisis Care works (256-236-0347 — they come to you, 24/7). Highland Health's crisis line works.

Everything else on the list at gatherstudio.app/steady has been called and verified.

## Email 3 — Day 4
**Subject:** What to say when you're not sure

---

One of the most paralyzing things about being the person next to someone who is struggling is not knowing whether what you're seeing is a crisis.

Here is what a chaplain at UAB told me when I was in the hospital: the bar for calling 988 is lower than you think. You do not have to be certain it's a crisis. You can call and say, "I'm not sure if this is an emergency, but I'm worried about my son/daughter/husband/friend, and I don't know what to do." The counselor will help you figure out what the next step is.

The same is true with Craig Crisis Care's mobile team. They don't just respond to emergencies. They respond to uncertainty.

The question I've found useful, for someone who is worried about a person they love: "Are you safe right now?" It is a direct question. It doesn't lead the person anywhere. It just asks what you most need to know.

If the answer is no, or "I don't know," or "I'm trying to be" — that is the moment to call.

Tomorrow: the week after the crisis.

— Matt

## Email 4 — Day 6
**Subject:** The week after

---

The crisis passes.

Not always cleanly. Not always in a way that feels like it's over. But the immediate danger passes, and then there is a different problem: figuring out what comes next.

This is the part the crisis resources don't cover. 988 will stay on the line. Craig Crisis Care will come to you. RMC and Highland have inpatient and outpatient care. But when your person is home and stable and trying to figure out what they are living with — that is where the week after starts.

What I've found on the list:
- Therapists in Calhoun County accepting new patients (some with Medicaid)
- The peer support group that still meets on Tuesdays
- Faith-based counseling programs run through local churches, for people of any tradition or none
- Programs specifically for veterans (VA Oxford clinic)

The directory is the map for the week after. Everything on it has been called.

Tomorrow: the one document I wish I'd had.

— Matt
