# The Better Business Bureau Gave Talkspace an A+. The Customers Gave It a 1.2.
*Southern Legends · southernlegends.blog*
*By Matt Headley*
*Category: Mental Health + NE Alabama*
*Status: DRAFT — voice check required before publish*

---

There is a gap between what a company says about itself and what the people who used it say.

Talkspace has an A+ rating from the Better Business Bureau. It also has a 1.2 out of 5 from the customers who reviewed it there, based on 313 complaints filed in three years.

The complaints are not about bad therapy. They are about being billed for care that was never delivered. Sessions scheduled without the patient's knowledge. Insurance claims that sat unprocessed for months. Support lines that did not answer.

That gap is not an accident. It is what happens when the company's actual customer is your employer, not you.

---

Here is how the Talkspace model works.

Talkspace started as a direct-to-consumer teletherapy service. Over time, it shifted toward Employee Assistance Programs — corporate contracts where the employer buys therapy access for employees in bulk, as a benefit.

In that model, the person sitting across from the therapist (virtually) is not really the customer. They are the end user. The customer is the HR department that signed the contract.

This creates a specific kind of institutional pressure. The goal is to look like a functional mental health benefit, not to make sure every person who needed a session got one.

---

The Better Business Bureau A+ is an accreditation grade, not a review score. It is based on how the company responds to complaints, not whether customers are satisfied. Talkspace responds to its BBB complaints. The response is often a variation of "we've resolved this" without the customer saying they agree.

A 1.2 customer review score, across 313 complaints, concentrated on billing and scheduling failures — that is the signal worth reading.

---

I want to be fair to Talkspace. There are people who have had good experiences with it. There are therapists on the platform who are good at what they do. Teletherapy as a model is real and has helped people who otherwise could not get to an office.

But when I built the Steady directory, I was not building it to serve someone who has a corporate EAP benefit and wants to use a national platform.

I was building it for the person in Calhoun County who does not have a corporate job with an EAP benefit. Who is Medicaid-eligible or uninsured. Who needs to know what is actually in their county and what will actually answer.

The list is at [gatherstudio.app/steady](https://gatherstudio.app/steady).

If you used Talkspace and your experience was bad, I believe you.

If you have a resource in NE Alabama that should be on the list — a therapist, a peer group, a faith-based counseling program — tell me. I will verify it and add it.
