# Chief Ladiga Trail — Full Transcript & Summary

## Summary

Matt shares his personal history with the Chief Ladiga Trail, a 40-mile railroad-to-trails conversion that runs through Calhoun and Cleburne counties in Alabama, from Anniston to the Georgia state line.

**Key themes:**
- **College (JSU):** First encounter with the trail while living in an apartment adjacent to it. Used it for running and walking, called it "Wadiga" initially.
- **Ministry years (Weaver, 6.5 years):** The trail became his refuge during the hardest season—a place to run while listening to sermon podcasts, to cry, to walk with Heather during their dating years, to teach children to ride bikes, to brainstorm and process ministry struggles.
- **Farm years (2021 onward):** Excitement when the trail extension south of Michael Tucker Park finally opened. Walked the railroad bed during construction, ran it when manic, walked it when depressed. Experienced the full spectrum of his bipolar condition on the trail.
- **The manic campaign (2023):** Conceived a multi-year bicentennial campaign celebrating Chief Ladiga and the Muskogee Creek Nation, marking 200 years since Chief Ladiga sold the land to the U.S. government in 1832—the same year his farmhouse was built. The idea was to partner with the city, the rails-to-trails program, and Native leadership to tell the real history of displacement beneath the trail's name.
- **Coming down:** Felt the idea died when forced to sell the farm, but now recognizes it may not be too late—six years remain until 2032.
- **Current direction:** Plan to talk to Toby (a local connection who works on trail projects) about connecting with the Muskogee Creek Nation to explore collaboration. The piece about his personal trail experience will serve as the launchpad for that conversation.
- **The bigger story:** A manic dream that didn't die, just got redirected. Also: reversals (he once ran while kids biked; now he'll ride his bike while his son runs). Visual anchors: old photos of kids on trainer bikes on the trail, the "tree tunnel" section, running the Woodstock 5K with twins in a double stroller.
