The burning bus on U.S. Highway 78, Anniston, Alabama, May 14, 1961. The moment that changed the Civil Rights Movement.

On Mother's Day 1961, a Greyhound bus carrying Freedom Riders was firebombed outside Anniston, Alabama. The riders — Black and white Americans testing federal desegregation law — were beaten and left choking in smoke. The burning bus became one of the most iconic photographs of the Civil Rights era.

"We will not stop. We have come too far to turn back now."

These shirts carry that moment. The woodcut design and the portrait of David Dennis, a Freedom Rider who stood in Anniston that day, are printed as a permanent act of witness.

David Dennis, Freedom Rider, Anniston 1961

David Dennis rode into Anniston knowing the risk. He went on to help organize Mississippi's Freedom Summer in 1964. His eulogy for James Chaney — one of three civil rights workers murdered that summer — is considered one of the most powerful speeches of the era.

These shirts honor that courage. And every dollar goes back to the community where it happened.
