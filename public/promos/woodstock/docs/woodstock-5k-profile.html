The Facts

The Woodstock 5K started in 1981. It has not missed a year since. That makes it one of the oldest continuously run footraces in the Southeast, and in 2026 it runs for the 45th time.

The full name now is the Publix Woodstock 5K, run alongside the Kidstock 1K. It goes off Saturday, August 1, 2026, from 7:30 to 10:30 in the morning, through the neighborhoods along Woodstock Avenue near Anniston High School. The Anniston Runners Club hosts it. Volunteers own it.

The 2026 race is the RRCA Alabama State 5K Championship. Over its run, seven national championships have been decided on Woodstock Avenue, along with five regional and six state titles. More than a thousand runners are expected.

The money stays here. Proceeds go to the Arc of Calhoun and Cleburne Counties, which serves people with disabilities, and to local schools and organizations.

What It Has Outlasted

The street itself is a clue. Anniston was founded in 1872 as a company town built by the Woodstock Iron Company — Samuel Noble and Daniel Tyler's furnace operation. Woodstock Avenue carries that name. The race runs, in effect, down a road named for the iron that built the town.

Then the town lost most of what came after. The furnaces cooled. The textile and pipe work thinned out across the twentieth century. Fort McClellan — the Army post that anchored Anniston for eight decades — closed in 1999. The Monsanto years left their own mark on the water and the name.

Through all of it, the first Saturday of August, people kept running down Woodstock Avenue.

Merch

If you run Woodstock, you already run trails. The shirts pair the race with the Chief Ladiga Trail — 33.5 miles of old rail bed from Anniston to the Georgia line, the closest long run most people around here have.

  • Woodstock Avenue Oval — cream tee, the race and the date, for the morning itself.
  • I Survived Woodstock Avenue — the August-in-Alabama badge of honor.
  • Ladiga's Land (Chief Ladiga Trail) — the trail shirt that goes with the training, not just the race.
